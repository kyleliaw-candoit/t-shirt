# Executive Summary

This repository documents the creation of an **AI-native commerce company**.

The initial product is **print-on-demand apparel**, but this repository is **not** about selling T-shirts.

It is about building a company that learns how to identify, understand, and serve communities through AI-assisted workflows.

Our first products happen to be T-shirts because they are an inexpensive and fast way to validate business ideas. The long-term goal is to build brands that authentically serve communities—not simply sell products.

Our guiding belief is:

> **We sell identity signaling, not T-shirts.**

---

# Why This Project Exists

Many print-on-demand businesses begin by asking:

> "What T-shirt should we make?"

We ask a different question:

> **"What community are we serving?"**

People rarely buy a shirt because they need another shirt.

They buy it because it communicates something about who they are.

Examples include:

* Jeep owners
* National Park enthusiasts
* Matcha lovers
* Mechanical keyboard enthusiasts
* Bird watchers

The product is simply the vehicle.

The identity is the product.

---

# Long-Term Vision

Build an **AI-native company** where AI assists with nearly every business function while the founder focuses on:

* strategy
* learning
* experimentation
* decision making

The goal is **not** to automate everything.

The goal is to automate the right things.

We intentionally avoid solving difficult automation problems before they become business bottlenecks.

---

# Company Philosophy

Our company follows several core principles.

## 1. Understand First. Automate Second.

Every workflow should be understood before it is automated.

We perform work manually first.

Only after understanding the process do we decide what AI should automate.

---

## 2. Build. Learn. Refine. Then Automate.

We do not attempt to design perfect systems upfront.

Instead we:

1. Build something useful.
2. Learn from real-world execution.
3. Improve the methodology.
4. Automate mature workflows.

---

## 3. Good Enough to Learn Beats Perfect Enough to Admire.

The objective is continuous learning, not perfect planning.

We deliberately avoid premature optimization.

---

## 4. Solve Today's Bottleneck.

Future architecture matters.

Today's execution matters more.

We intentionally postpone complexity until it is earned.

---

## 5. Every Decision Must Earn Its Place.

Every task should answer:

> **"So what?"**

If a task does not improve a business decision, it probably should not be performed.

---

## 6. Assume Nothing. Earn Every Conclusion With Evidence.

Business decisions should be supported by evidence.

The company separates:

* Evidence
* Interpretation
* Decision

This keeps reasoning transparent and continually improvable.

# AI Organization

Rather than thinking of AI as a single assistant, the company is designed around specialized AI roles.

Current roles include:

### Community Analyst

Researches communities and produces structured research reports.

Responsible for:

* community analysis
* scoring
* identifying identity boundaries
* documenting evidence

The Community Analyst **does not** make branding decisions.

---

### Strategy Advisor

Uses research reports to recommend which communities are worth pursuing.

Responsible for:

* evaluating opportunities
* portfolio thinking
* strategic prioritization

---

### Brand Manager

Develops long-term brand direction for selected communities.

Responsible for:

* brand positioning
* messaging
* brand identity

---

### Creative Director

Turns strategy into products.

Responsible for:

* design briefs
* collections
* creative concepts

---

### Knowledge Librarian

Maintains company knowledge.

Responsible for:

* updating documentation
* recording validated learnings
* improving operating methodologies

---

# Community Research Methodology

Every potential community is evaluated using the same framework.

## Community Score

Measures whether the community is worth pursuing.

Current criteria include:

* Identity Strength
* Identity Expression Potential
* Brand Expansion Potential
* Differentiation Potential

---

## Creative Leverage

Measures how much long-term creative opportunity naturally exists within the community.

Examples include:

* insider language
* symbols
* rituals
* recurring themes
* visual motifs

---

## Founder Advantage

Evaluates how prepared the founder currently is to authentically serve the community.

This score is proposed by AI but ultimately approved by the founder.

---

# Community Analyst Responsibilities

The Community Analyst's job is to understand communities—not design brands.

Responsibilities include:

* collecting evidence
* identifying identity communities
* identifying identity fault lines
* identifying adjacent communities
* determining whether a research scope should be divided into multiple communities

The Community Analyst intentionally stops before making:

* branding decisions
* positioning decisions
* product decisions

Those belong to later AI roles.
