# MVV #1 Brand Review Checklist

## 1. Used By / Referenced By

### Primary user

**Brand Manager**
- Uses this checklist for the final pre-launch brand review.
- Classifies problems as launch-blocking or nonblocking.

### Referenced by

**Creative Director**
- Uses the Meta section before handing acquisition creative to Brand Manager.
- Uses the product-fit questions when reviewing the LP assortment.

**MVV Landing-Page Developer**
- Uses the LP section as a self-check before requesting Brand Manager review.

**Performance-marketing / MVV operator**
- Uses the experiment-interpretation section after launch to avoid conflating acquisition and product evidence.

**Strategy Advisor**
- May reference post-test interpretation where findings affect the commercial hypothesis.
- Does not need to approve routine Brand Manager execution.

# Part A — Meta Acquisition Creative

## Correct Customer

- [ ] Would this mean something specifically to an antiques / vintage treasure hunter?
- [ ] Is the recognition based on behavior, identity, discernment, or insider truth rather than only generic old-object imagery?
- [ ] Are we likely attracting R012 customers rather than generic curiosity?

## Fast Communication

- [ ] Can the core idea be understood during a fast mobile scroll?
- [ ] Is essential copy readable at realistic small-screen presentation?
- [ ] Does the creative contain enough information to earn attention without requiring prolonged inspection?

**Important:** These requirements apply to the acquisition execution, not to every T-shirt product.

## Category Decoding

- [ ] Can the viewer connect the execution to antiques / vintage discovery / flea markets / estate sales / treasure hunting?
- [ ] If the brand name appears, does the rest of the ad provide enough context to decode “Worth the Detour” correctly?
- [ ] Is it clear enough that apparel is being presented or promoted?

## Brand Meaning

- [ ] Does the execution connect to “I see what other people miss” or a credible supporting recognition mechanism?
- [ ] Does it preserve finding + discernment + story?
- [ ] Does it feel observant/knowing rather than elitist?
- [ ] Does humor, if present, recognize actual hunter behavior?

## Drift Check

- [ ] Not generic vintage aesthetics.
- [ ] Not generic thrift-fashion identity.
- [ ] Not farmhouse/rustic novelty.
- [ ] Not reseller hustle.
- [ ] Not appraisal/connoisseur authority.
- [ ] Not generic nostalgia.
- [ ] Not generic travel/wanderlust.

## Acquisition Review Decision

Choose one:

- [ ] **Ready**
- [ ] **Ready with nonblocking observations**
- [ ] **Revise before launch**
- [ ] **Brand-positioning blocker**

Record only consequential notes.

# Part B — Landing Page

## Immediate Orientation

- [ ] Can visitors quickly tell this is an apparel brand?
- [ ] Can visitors quickly understand the initial world is antiques / vintage treasure hunting / overlooked finds?
- [ ] Is “For people who see what others miss” or equivalent identity meaning present in the system?
- [ ] Does the page avoid forcing visitors to read a long brand explanation?

## Product Assortment

- [ ] Are products included because they are credible Worth the Detour products rather than merely because they make good ads?
- [ ] Have strong-product / weak-ad candidates been given a fair opportunity to appear?
- [ ] Does the assortment collectively express more than one dimension of the brand?
- [ ] Does each product fit the R012 entry wedge?
- [ ] Have unrelated future “X is worth the detour” adjacencies been kept out of MVV #1?

## Product Meaning

For each included product, confirm at least one meaningful mechanism:

- [ ] Hunter's eye / discernment.
- [ ] Hunt behavior.
- [ ] Story of the find.
- [ ] Rescue / worth saving.
- [ ] Insider humor / obsession.
- [ ] Another defensible R012 recognition mechanism.

Reject or question products whose only justification is “it looks vintage.”

## Fair Presentation

- [ ] Can each important design be seen clearly enough to understand?
- [ ] Are more detailed concepts large enough to inspect?
- [ ] Are text-heavy designs readable in LP presentation?
- [ ] Is any major presentation advantage intentional rather than accidental?
- [ ] Are products not being penalized merely because they were poor small-screen ad objects?

## Copy / Voice

- [ ] Observant and curious.
- [ ] Knowing without snobbery.
- [ ] Self-aware where appropriate.
- [ ] No fake expertise.
- [ ] No generic vintage-brand poetry.
- [ ] No generic wanderlust language.
- [ ] No unsupported product, availability, scarcity, or launch claims.

## LP Review Decision

Choose one:

- [ ] **Ready**
- [ ] **Ready with nonblocking observations**
- [ ] **Revise before launch**
- [ ] **Brand/testing blocker**

# Part C — Experiment Interpretation

After MVV #1, do not create one undifferentiated design leaderboard.

## Acquisition Evidence

Ask:

> **Which executions efficiently earned relevant attention?**

Use acquisition performance, CTR and related acquisition signals, and incoming creative attribution.

Do **not** automatically call these the best T-shirts.

## Product-Intent Evidence

Ask:

> **Which actual products generated desire once visitors could inspect them?**

Use meaningful product exposure, product engagement, product-specific intent, and lead behavior associated with product interest.

Do not penalize a product for weak Meta performance if it received a fair LP test.

## Purchase Evidence

MVV #1 does **not** identify purchase winners.

Customers cannot complete a purchase in this experiment. Actual willingness to pay requires later real-money validation.

## Possible Result Classes

A design may emerge as:

- [ ] strong acquisition creative + strong product;
- [ ] strong acquisition creative + weak product;
- [ ] weak acquisition creative + strong product;
- [ ] weak acquisition creative + weak product;
- [ ] insufficient evidence.

None of these combinations is inherently contradictory.

## Brand-Learning Questions

After results are available, Brand Manager should ask:

- [ ] Which recognition mechanisms attracted relevant attention?
- [ ] Which recognition mechanisms produced downstream product intent?
- [ ] Do the same mechanisms win both jobs?
- [ ] Which products appear desirable despite weak acquisition suitability?
- [ ] Did generic category messages outperform deeper hunter-eye recognition?
- [ ] Did any apparent winner succeed for reasons inconsistent with Worth the Detour?
- [ ] Did the market reveal a material contradiction in the approved brand hypothesis?

Only the final question, or another similarly material contradiction, should trigger reconsideration of upstream strategy.

---

# Final Brand Manager Launch Rule

> **Use scroll-legible creative to acquire relevant attention. Use the landing page to discover which products customers actually want. Preserve Worth the Detour's hunter-eye identity in both, but do not force the same execution to perform both jobs.**

Block launch only when a material brand or experiment-integrity problem would corrupt the learning.

Do not delay MVV over polish.
