# Representative Marketplace Evidence — Initial Scan

## R006 — Sourdough People

Current Etsy category results show **5,000+ sourdough/baking shirt items**. Highly repeated visible concepts include:

- “Don’t Get Me Started” / starter wordplay,
- “In My Sourdough Era,”
- “Wake and Bake,”
- bread-maker / baker identity,
- starter-themed gifts,
- homestead / homemaking framing,
- generic bread graphics.

Visible listings include products with review counts in the thousands and substantial promotional discounting. This strongly validates identity-signaling apparel demand, but also shows that the obvious pun / era / bread-graphic territory is crowded.

**Initial strategic question:** can deeper process-recognition territory — starter caretaking, fermentation behavior, failure/recovery, technical competence, and authentic baker behavior — create meaningful differentiation beyond generic sourdough humor?

## R007 — Pickleball Players

Current Etsy category results show **5,000+ pickleball T-shirt items**. Highly repeated visible concepts include:

- “Dink” jokes and puns,
- “Dink responsibly,”
- memory / aging jokes,
- retirement framing,
- paddle graphics,
- player/team personalization,
- generic pickleball obsession.

Multiple visible listings show review counts in the thousands, with heavy discounting and low-price competition.

The market also contains serious dedicated performance-apparel competition. Centerline Athletics has been positioned as a major pickleball apparel brand, with partnerships across USA Pickleball and major professional-tour organizations, reported rapid sales growth, and expanded retail distribution. This creates competition at both ends: commoditized novelty merchandise and increasingly sophisticated sport-specific apparel.

**Initial strategic question:** is there a differentiated identity territory between low-cost novelty humor and premium performance apparel that is strong enough to justify a test, especially given Founder Advantage of 2/10?
