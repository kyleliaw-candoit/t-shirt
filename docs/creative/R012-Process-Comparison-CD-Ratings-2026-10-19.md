## Evaluation conventions

- Primary score: humor 1–5, half points and ties allowed. 1 = no functioning joke; 2 = recognizable idea with little comic payoff; 3 = mild amusement or competent but familiar turn; 4 = clear, strong comic turn worth developing; 5 = exceptional immediate comic payoff for the intended audience. Half points lie between these anchors. These are qualitative judgments, not interval measurements.
- Evaluate copy plus the specified essential visual, without relying on source or mechanism explanations. Assume readable basic execution, not artwork that repairs a weak premise.
- Advance yes / maybe / no is a separate provisional shirt-development judgment, not a predicted purchase. A warm concept can merit consideration despite a lower humor score.
- Confidence concerns the editorial assessment of the written concept. No audience ratings or rendered-artwork results exist.
- The evaluator authored the candidates, knows the process assignments, and has seen earlier Founder feedback (3/5 self-control, 4/5 measured with heart, 3/5 selling childhood). This is a precommitted assessment before ratings on these 24, not an independent or fully blind evaluation.
- Do not force a winner for each material, equal scores across methods, or agreement with the Founder.
- Keep this original record unchanged when Founder scores arrive. Record their scores and any subsequent revised judgments separately, with reasons. Do not retroactively improve copy before comparing.

## Comparison after Founder ratings

Collect all four groups before revealing these ratings or method-level preferences. Preserve original scores. Compare concept by concept within each material: exact agreement, differences of half a point, larger gaps, and the ordering of favorites. A consistent overall scoring offset may reflect use of the scale rather than different taste. Differences in ranking and shirt-advance judgments are more consequential.

Discuss the largest gaps using each person's reading of the joke, recognition burden, surprise, tone and wearability. Neither rater is assumed correct. If explanation changes a rating, record both the initial and revised judgment rather than replacing the first impression. Do not average the two sets into a presumed objective truth.

Compare approaches separately for each rater across all four materials. Distinguish material-specific strengths, peak scores and consistency among the selected finalists. A threshold of 4/5 can serve as the previously proposed working benchmark, not a statistical success test. Avoid declaring a method reliable from this small, shared-author exercise.

This record supports explicit preference calibration in future prompts and documentation; it does not modify model weights or establish customer demand. No methodology amendment, merged PR, artwork approval or market validation is implied.
