# TRM-002 — Formal record for an informal experience

**In plain language:** use a familiar system of recording, judging or authorizing things to describe an experience it would not normally govern.

**Creative payoff hypothesis:** make an everyday habit feel ceremonially recognized, expose an absurdity, or express an affectionate self-justification.

## Essential ingredients

1. Recognizable formal cues from a particular system: for example labeled fields, an award category, a rating convention or a status declaration.
2. A subject or function that is unexpected for that system.
3. Content that uses the system's logic to reveal a meaningful attitude, rather than simply decorating the subject with a border.
4. Enough legibility for the reader to recognize both the borrowed convention and its changed use.

**Adjustable:** system, experience, tone, fields, and verdict. A review evaluates; an award recognizes; an application requests permission; a certificate declares status. Choose the function deliberately instead of treating these as interchangeable graphic skins.

**Optional:** simulated age, ornamental border, official-looking texture and exact palette. **Not optional:** whichever cues make the selected convention recognizable. Real institutional logos or seals are unnecessary.

## Identity fit and creation

**Fits when:** the identity group knows the borrowed convention and recognizes the everyday experience being subjected to it. State what judgment, permission or recognition the wearer would enjoy.

**Fails when:** the format is used normally without an unexpected application. A dealer's ordinary price tag on an ordinary antique is a price tag, not evidence of this operation. A vintage-looking frame alone also fails.

Choose an audience behavior, select a familiar system that would amusingly record or govern it, and write two or three fields using that system's logic. Keep the strongest mismatch; remove fields that merely add decorative density.

**Neighboring operations:** honorary identity labels and permission for excess. Those can operate through words alone; this recipe also relies on format recognition. A concept can validly use both. There is no requirement to prove that only this recipe explains it.

## Illustrative application — not source evidence

**Antique/vintage treasure hunters:** a small household application reads “Request for shelf space / Item: unexpected / Reason: look at it,” with a circled approval. Administrative justification is applied to the emotional recognition of one compelling find.

This is review sketch B2. It assumes that affectionate mock documentation of discovery fits the audience. The request fields and approval convey the operation; antique-looking paper is unnecessary. Avoid framing the wearer as unable to manage money or possessions.
