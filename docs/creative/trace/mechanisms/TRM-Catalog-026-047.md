## TRM-026 — Consumer flavor convention applied to incompatible goods

v1.0 · CANDIDATE · Families: F05

**Operation:** Apply a familiar flavor or seasonal product convention to a category where it is absurd.

**Proposed payoff:** recognition of the linked relationship, with surprise or self-recognition where the audience shares the premise. This is a hypothesis about appeal.

**Essential conditions**

1. Recognizable consumer convention.
2. Clearly incompatible product category.
3. Enough category cues for the collision.

**Fit and failure:** Fails if the product could plausibly be an ordinary flavored item.

**Adjustable:** audience, setting, wording and visual style, provided every condition above survives. **Conditional execution:** retain the sound, reading order, action, geometry, format or placement that supplies the evidence; an ornamental change cannot substitute for it. Texture and embellishments can be removed only if recognition survives.

**Creation procedure:** identify an audience situation that satisfies these conditions; write the expected reading and changed reading/action; construct a fresh expression showing their connection; test whether another reader perceives it without explanation. Neighboring recipes can participate; no exclusivity test is required.

**Grounded sources and copying boundaries**

- [Source 95 — Seasonal emergency medicine](../pilots/TRACE-Etsy-120-Source-Decodes.md#source-95): Apply seasonal coffee flavor conventions to serious medical substances. Avoid reusing this source's combined wording, characters, props and arrangement; change the underlying situation, not only a noun or color.

**Illustrative application — unvalidated:** Give office supplies a seasonal tasting menu; avoid implying real product properties.

**Separate assessments:** the cited observations support this proposed relationship; grouping and portability remain interpretive. Commercial support is limited to source listing signals and the [credited pairs](../pilots/TRACE-Etsy-120-Evidence.json); there is no sales-causality claim.
