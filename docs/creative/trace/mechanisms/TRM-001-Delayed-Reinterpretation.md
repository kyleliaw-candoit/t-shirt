# TRM-001 — Delayed reinterpretation

**In plain language:** establish a familiar expectation, then reveal information that changes how the reader understands the first beat.

**Creative payoff hypothesis:** affectionate self-recognition, surprise, or permission to admit a familiar contradiction. Choose the payoff using the identity group's context.

## Essential ingredients

1. A setup that produces a specific, understandable expectation.
2. A later verbal or visual beat encountered after the setup.
3. A meaningful connection: the later beat reverses or qualifies that expectation, rather than merely adding unrelated surprise.

**Adjustable:** speaker, situation, expectation, reversal versus qualification, and verbal versus sequential visual expression. An illustrated sequence can do the work without a slogan.

**Optional:** mascot, decorative frame, texture and specific typeface. **Not optional:** the reading order and the connection between the two beats. Simultaneous placement is acceptable if the visual hierarchy still creates a reveal.

## Identity fit and creation

**Fits when:** an audience recognizes both the expected behavior and the revealing contradiction, and welcomes that self-portrait. Write the audience assumption separately from source evidence.

**Fails when:** the punchline could be attached to any opening without changing its meaning. Example: a competence claim followed by an unrelated animal image is surprise, but does not demonstrate delayed reinterpretation. It may work through another mechanism.

Start with one familiar audience situation. State the conventional expectation, decide what second fact changes it, then arrange two beats so the audience can make that connection unaided. Check tone against the brand and audience context.

**Neighboring operations:** simultaneous claim/image contradiction and deadpan shocking disclosure. A concept may use these alongside the reveal. No exclusive family assignment is required; judge whether the intended operation is present.
