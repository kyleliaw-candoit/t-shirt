# R012 — Fashion-First Assortment Learning

## Decision

For the current pre-MVV collection-building round, Worth the Detour will deliberately include a small number of **fashion-first / scroll-stop designs** alongside the more authentic, insider, behavioral, and identity-led locked designs.

The goal is not to replace the deeper creative strategy. The goal is to improve the **assortment as a whole** and create stronger entry points for mobile social discovery.

---

## Why This Matters

Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and similar feeds are consumed on small screens under fast, casual scrolling conditions.

Many of Worth the Detour's strongest authentic designs reward a closer look. That is valuable once a customer is browsing deliberately, but it may not always be the strongest first-frame attention mechanism.

Therefore, some shirts can be designed primarily to:

- stop the scroll,
- look excellent at thumbnail size,
- create visual variety in an ad carousel,
- earn a store visit,
- and give the customer a chance to discover the deeper authentic products afterward.

Working shorthand:

> **Some designs earn the click; other designs earn the customer.**

---

## Current R012 Priority for Fashion-First Designs

For this specific round, prioritize in this order:

1. **Great T-shirt / fashion design**
2. **Scroll-stop strength**
3. **Wearability and visual desire**
4. **Enough antique / vintage / treasure-hunting connection to belong**
5. **Deep authenticity or insider meaning, only when it improves rather than constrains the shirt**

A great shirt with a weak but credible category cue is preferred over a merely good shirt with an obvious category cue.

---

## Category-Cue Strategy

The antique / vintage connection does not need to dominate the graphic.

It can be established through a small supporting element such as:

- a concise line of copy,
- `ANTIQUES`, `ESTATE`, `FOUND`, `BOOTH`, or similar notation,
- a catalog / inventory mark,
- a subtle vintage object,
- a dealer / archival cue,
- a secondary phrase such as `ANTIQUES ARE WORTH THE DETOUR`.

This allows the primary graphic to operate more freely as fashion.

---

## Evaluation Standard

For these fashion-first candidates, the Creative Director should ask:

- Does this look like a genuinely good designer T-shirt even before the niche meaning is understood?
- Does it read quickly on a small phone screen?
- Is there one strong dominant visual idea?
- Would it add useful visual contrast to the existing locked portfolio?
- Is it eye-catching without becoming cheap-looking or disposable?
- Does the antique / vintage cue feel sufficient but not forced?
- Would we still be proud to sell the product if it became the customer's first exposure to Worth the Detour?

Do not penalize a strong fashion-first design merely because its identity insight is shallow. In this lane, shallowness can be intentional if the shirt performs its merchandising role exceptionally well.

---

## Portfolio Intent

The desired Worth the Detour assortment should not feel like every design was generated from the same creative mechanism.

A healthy mix can include:

- deep insider recognition,
- behavioral humor,
- quiet discernment,
- story / provenance,
- subtle identity signals,
- and a limited number of louder, fashion-led, scroll-stop products.

The fashion-first pieces should create energy and visual range without displacing the authentic core.

---

## Relationship to MVV

The immediate goal is to add **two strong fashion-first designs** to the current locked portfolio before beginning MVV.

When interpreting later results, remember that these products may be performing a different funnel job from deeper identity-led shirts. Initial attention strength should not automatically be interpreted as stronger community authenticity, and slower-burning authentic designs should not be rejected solely because they are less visually aggressive in a feed.
