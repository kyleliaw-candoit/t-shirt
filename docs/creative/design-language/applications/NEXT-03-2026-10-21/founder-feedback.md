## Creative Director response and calibration

**T-E:** The second execution is a useful distinct preference test within the one/two cap. The exact timing joke already supplies the purchase-before-logistics reversal. A small old cabinet with a dealer tag can anchor the antique/secondhand setting without asking a miniature car/cabinet scene to explain it. This relaxes the original scene-led brief intentionally, following Founder direction; the approved Playful design remains intact. See [TE-quiet-02](TE-quiet-02/README.md).

**N-A:** The antique/vintage connection is plausible but suggestive, not unambiguous. The worn striped household bowl, inexpensive price, seller reference and period valuation-card treatment jointly favor a secondhand-find interpretation. The central meaning—private emotional value exceeds the purchase price—is clear to my reading. However, an unfamiliar viewer might read general thrifting, collecting or sentimental shopping rather than antique hunting specifically. Vintage styling alone is not proof of audience recognition. This is a creative inference from the actual art, not a finding from customer testing.

Recommendation: retain the current candidate without an automatic redesign. Its broader secondhand/collecting interpretation is compatible with the approved positioning; niche connection need not be exclusive to work. For a small future recognition check, show the shirt without the research or brand explanation and ask what kind of purchase/person it brings to mind. If the recurring answer is generic shopping and misses found/old objects, then consider one concrete estate-sale/dealer cue. Do not report that recognition check as completed or add copy before deciding that it improves the shirt.

**T-B:** My prior assessment advanced it as a quieter third candidate. Founder rejects it at the concept level; the earlier positive judgment remains visible rather than being rewritten. Retire it. We have not tried all eight families, so this decision does not establish that every family is incapable of expressing it. The practical learning is that a respectable original copy rating and a plausible visual treatment do not obligate continued investment when the resulting shirt fails the concept review.

**Application-specific lesson:** T-E names a concrete question and an explicit timing reversal; T-B leaves “how” underspecified and asks more of its image. Test how much semantic work the visual must perform before choosing a small supporting graphic. This is a useful contrast from this round, not proof that typography-led designs generally outperform illustration-led ones.

## Follow-up clarification — audience breadth

Founder clarified during this work that thrift-shopping and sentimental collecting are fully compatible with the brand. For N-A, the relevant condition is that the object artwork clearly suggests an old object; viewers need not identify formal antiques or antique hunting specifically. This resolves the narrower category-recognition concern above.

CD reinspection: the bowl's mottled, worn-looking surface, softened striped decoration, traditional profile and foot give it an old, previously used character. The aged quality is carried by the object as well as the surrounding card. It is sufficient to my eye for this review; no extra ANTIQUES or ESTATE SALE label is needed. Keep the current artwork. The Founder has not reviewed a changed N-A image because none was created.

The earlier proposed recognition question should therefore check whether the object reads as old/found/previously used, not demand the answer “antiques.” Thrifting and sentimental-collecting responses count as compatible interpretations. This clarification is also recorded in the brand scope and R012 creative learnings.
