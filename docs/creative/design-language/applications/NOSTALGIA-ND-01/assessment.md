# Frozen Creative Director assessment — NOSTALGIA-ND-01

October 21, 2026 (America/Chicago) · Recorded after artwork and controlled local shirt inspection, before Founder feedback on this application. Append later feedback; do not replace this assessment. Provider evidence and any resulting changes belong in separate dated additions.

**Recommendation:** present both for creative review. Prefer **B / Framed artifact / Pepper** for clear emotional storytelling; retain **A / Heritage / Ivory** as the more object-led, open composition. Neither is production-ready. This is a comparative taste judgment, not a demand forecast.

## Concept judgment, kept separate from visual execution

N-D is a warm concept, not a humor-led one. “I didn't buy a bowl. I bought Saturday at Grandma's.” makes the emotional category shift understandable without the source story. A newly acquired bowl evokes one's own memory; it need not be Grandma's original possession. Warmth is strong to my reading; humor is low and not the goal. The existing Founder 4.5/5 is a reported concept-appeal score, not measured humor or shirt purchase intent. Broad recognition remains a provisional E005 hypothesis with dependent voices on one source page.

## A — Heritage illustrated emblem, selected v3

- **Intent / actual result:** an object cherished beyond its price, with serif framing, blue/cream drawing and rust copy. The bowl is recognizable and the sale tag is visible close up. The visual reads as a memory held by the bowl, but the people also appear literally inside a giant bowl. That ambiguity is the main semantic weakness; the copy supports the intended reading but cannot guarantee it.
- **Relationships:** the original v1 crowded the arch and lower caption. V2 improved upper separation but left the bowl base too close to the footer. V3 reduces the central group and separates both bands without increasing the total footprint. The lower gap is still tighter than the open arch area, but the footer no longer touches the bowl optically. The heavy slab-like serif is more emphatic than the moderate classic voice requested. It remains coherent, though less delicate than the approved CAR Heritage application.
- **Two scales:** enlarged artwork retains warm expressions and material detail. At whole-shirt scale, the bowl, two figures and AT GRANDMA'S survive; the fine faces, tag string and hatching do not all remain distinct. The specific secondhand cue is weaker at distance. Upper arc, small central image and strong footer make three separated beats; further shrinking would weaken the emotional scene.
- **Footprint / color:** select compact 8.75 × 8.3794 inches over medium 10.25 × 9.8159. Medium improves detail but adds coverage without resolving the literal-bowl ambiguity. Ivory gives rust/blue good separation and a warm context. Pepper/Moss weaken rust-copy contrast; Blue Jean is less warm and reduces blue separation. Keep one colorway.
- **Wearability:** moderate-to-strong for a wearer drawn to affectionate vintage kitchenware. More restrained than B in height and ink area, but it may feel like sentimental merchandise to someone without a personal bowl memory. Do not infer universal gender preference from the grandmother/child scene.
- **Reference comparison:** DLS-HISTOREE-011 and DLS-ETSY-022 carry object context between serif heading and lower anchor; the preserved cards/details informed the open perimeter and hierarchy. A's narrative is more demanding than their single heroes. The source image relationships do not prove transfer or excuse the ambiguity. CAR round05 supplies an application-specific spacing comparison, not a mandatory arch template.
- **Disposition:** credible alternate for review; if Founder finds the people-in-bowl reading distracting, revise the visual metaphor rather than explain it away. Do not make another spacing variant without that feedback.

## B — Framed illustrated artifact, selected v1

- **Intent / actual result:** a keepsake recipe/storybook illustration. Grandmother guiding the child's stirring hand is immediately more concrete than A's memory inset. A used bowl with hanging tag connects the family scene to acquisition; the tag remains invented staging, not proof of original ownership.
- **Relationships:** frame, title field, counter and footer belong to one illustrative system. Cream outlines and sage/terracotta forms separate well on Pepper. The figures are intentionally nested inside the frame; Grandma's hair approaches the title, but it does not obscure the words. This is acceptable containment to my eye, rather than automatically applying Heritage's open gaps. The border's repetitive rules and filled caption fields add visual weight.
- **Two scales:** close up, faces/hands tell the shared baking action more convincingly than A. At shirt scale the two figures, bowl and strong footer survive; the upper title is the weaker read because it is small and compressed. From farther away the shirt says family baking before secondhand treasure hunting. At conversational distance the text and tag complete that connection. A cold viewer may still categorize it as a baking/grandmother shirt; identity transfer is promising, not demonstrated.
- **Footprint / color:** select compact 8.25 × 10.401 inches over medium 9.5 × 11.9769. The medium improves title size but becomes a substantial torso panel; compact retains a coherent card without drifting as far toward a poster. Pepper is the strongest contrast and most collected-looking context. Moss is plausible but sage shapes merge somewhat; Ivory weakens cream boundaries; Blue Jean is cooler and less integrated. Keep one colorway.
- **Wearability:** visually stronger emotional storytelling than A, with a more decorative, family-album character. It will likely appeal to a narrower aesthetic subgroup. I prefer it for this concept, while recognizing that someone who dislikes framed front art may prefer A or neither.
- **Reference comparison:** DLS-ETSY-016 demonstrates integrated storybook image, boundary and title; DLS-ZZ-029 shows cloth visible through a framed illustration. This application changes the content and mood, avoiding their distinctive subjects, captions and gothic cues. It retains the family's substantial-height and border risks rather than treating a frame as automatic quality.
- **Disposition:** preferred creative direction for review. If advanced, retain the emotional scene and improve native detail/print robustness before release. Printful advises against perfect borders because physical printing can warp them; the frame therefore needs sample judgment.

## Common limits and assessment integrity

- Native effective detail at selected width: A about **132.2 ppi**, B about **113.0 ppi**. A 150-DPI canvas export does not restore missing detail. Fine engraving, partly transparent edges and small lettering need a production derivative; no production-quality claim is made.
- Both use 1717 because washed, relaxed character supports the antique/keepsake context; no meaningful conflict justifies switching to 64000. Audience aesthetic fit remains a hypothesis.
- Local transform is explicitly provisional, using cached provider blanks with the required color underlay. Photographed reference size and physical neckline distance are unknown. Both leave visible cloth below the collar and end above the lower torso in this simulation, not a physical fit verification.
- These executions differ in family, color, ink, composition and scene treatment. They are product alternatives, not a controlled causal experiment isolating visual family. Same wording/material is held fixed.
- Do not turn this judgment into a score composite or call either family validated. Founder feedback and later customer behavior can disagree with this assessment.
