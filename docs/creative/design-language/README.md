# Design-language recipe bank

Owner: Creative Director · Working approach agreed by Founder on October 19, 2026 (America/Chicago) · Recipe candidates provisional

## Purpose and boundaries

1. Create usable original T-shirt designs from audience material plus a creative concept.
2. Develop and evolve Creative Director taste/judgment through explicit assessments, feedback and further applications.

A TRACE mechanism recipe describes how a concept produces meaning, humor or emotion. A design-language recipe describes how visual choices work together on a garment. Keep their IDs and evidence distinct. Recipes guide decisions; they do not guarantee good concepts, good execution or sales.

Canonical workflow integration: [Creative Director Methodology §9](../../company/Creative-Director-Methodology.md#91-design-language-recipes-and-judgment). Reusable design judgment: [T-shirt Design Playbook](../../company/Creative-Director-Tshirt-Design-Playbook.md). This bank owns the visual records and discovery format; it does not replace either document.

## Image-native discovery, lightweight attributes

Study the images themselves first. Group examples by recurring combinations of visual relationships and garment treatment, then describe enough attributes to compare, retrieve and apply them. A recipe family can have fuzzy boundaries; it need not require an exact combination of numerical dimension scores. Numerical scoring or automated clustering is not a prerequisite. Keep boundary examples and uncertainty instead of forcing membership.

For each candidate family, maintain a visual recipe card containing:

- **Complete shirt reference:** preserve the whole impression, garment relationship and wearer context when available. Mark artwork-only captures; do not infer placement from them.
- **Annotated detail crops:** lettering, illustration style, texture and the interactions that make the design work. Crops remain linked to the full source; zoom does not create evidence of finer detail.
- **Measured visual relationships:** palette, approximate print proportions, spacing and relative text/image sizes. State the denominator for ratios and label mockup estimates; do not invent physical dimensions.
- **Neutral reconstruction:** generic wording and independently made placeholder shapes preserve the relationships being studied without reproducing distinctive artwork or captions. It tests our interpretation, not the source creator's intent.
- **Lightweight attributes:** aesthetic/era, typography character, graphic subject/style, palette/contrast, texture, composition, text/image balance, print size and placement. Describe specific visible characteristics rather than relying on labels such as “retro” alone; add attributes only when useful.
- **Garment relationship:** cut/silhouette, fabric appearance or listed specification, color, print interaction and body placement. Label each fact observed, listed, inferred or proposed. A mockup cannot verify actual fabric weight, composition, feel or fit.
- **Usable guidance:** essential versus flexible relationships; compatible concept needs; what copy and graphics must communicate; adaptation examples; failure conditions; source/application links; evidence limitations.

Preserve source provenance and the exact commercial signal (for example, seller-controlled best-seller collection placement). Such placement does not establish unit sales, persistence or a causal effect of the visual style. Separate observed source patterns from analyst hypotheses and new application choices. Learn relationships rather than copying distinctive source art or caption/layout combinations.

## Application and calibration

Select recipes according to the semantic work required by the material and concept, following the Playbook. Apply them to original content and inspect the actual shirt, including cut, fabric appearance, color, print scale and placement. Check whether a first-time viewer can understand the situation and identity connection without the research explanation.

Record the Creative Director's initial assessment before Founder feedback when calibrating judgment. Preserve original scores and reasons; append qualitative feedback, revised diagnosis and the next proposed change. Consider comprehension, audience connection, typography, illustration, color, composition, semantic hierarchy, garment integration, visual appeal and wearability. No one check or mechanical composite score replaces judgment. Founder taste is useful calibration, not customer purchase evidence.

Use new applications to test transfer and refine essential/flexible boundaries. A successful internal application is encouraging but does not prove repeatability. Keep candidate status explicit rather than automatically declaring a validated recipe after one favorable review. No new mandatory approval layer or fixed study quota is introduced.
