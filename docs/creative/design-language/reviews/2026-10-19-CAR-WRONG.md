# CAR: WRONG — recipe application and judgment calibration

Date: October 19, 2026 (America/Chicago) · Owner: Creative Director · Founder feedback received; recipe candidates provisional; visual revision pending

## Context

Material: R012-E007 transport constraints. Fixed concept: **HEIGHT: PERFECT. WIDTH: PERFECT. CAR: WRONG.** Mechanism: TRM-002. The research story involved a thrift-shop hat rack and a truck; cabinet/car/tag staging and artwork are invented. Both applications omit the earlier optional MEASUREMENTS heading. See the [R012 process comparison](../../R012-Process-Comparison-Results-2026-10-19.md).

The pilot tested two contrasting visual recipes after earlier executions felt poster-like to the Founder:

- **DLR-001, condensed emphasis stack:** compact condensed lettering, selective orange payoff accent, small cabinet/car illustration on charcoal.
- **DLR-002, open collegiate emblem:** arched collegiate lettering, larger cabinet/car illustration, lower text anchor, cream/teal on charcoal.

Source evidence came from retained [HISTOREE-002 records](../../trace/brand-studies/HISTOREE-002-Evidence.json), not a new scan. DLR-001 used H2-031, H2-022 and H2-043. DLR-002 used H2-009 (primary collegiate type), H2-017 (supporting emblem grammar); H2-069 was a closed-seal boundary example, not a family member. These sources are one seller's best-selling collection, not sales totals or independent commercial validation.

## Founder feedback — appended October 19

The following is a faithful paraphrase; Founder supplied no numeric ratings for this round:

1. Both designs are much better T-shirt designs than the prior executions, and both are proper/good T-shirt designs rather than posters.
2. In design #2, the first two lines should have equal visual weight. Alternatively combine them into **“Width & Height: PERFECT.”** Catching this imperfection is an example of the taste/judgment the Founder wants the CD to develop.
3. Matching hierarchy to meaning/rhythm is important but explicitly **not the only judgment** to apply.
4. Both designs have strengths and weaknesses. Design #1's dependence on copy/typography suits materials and concepts that do not need visual cues to convey meaning or identity connection. For this particular concept, its less-prominent graphics increase the first-time viewer's risk of asking what the copy is about.
5. Founder agreed that recipe selection should depend on what copy communicates independently and what essential information graphics must supply; the actual shirt must work without our research explanation.

## Revised CD interpretation — not a replacement of initial scores

- The initial preference for #1's breathing room overemphasized restraint relative to the graphic's explanatory job. “HEIGHT/WIDTH/CAR” leaves the object and situation unstated; a tiny icon can hide essential information.
- DLR-002 is the stronger starting point for this particular visually dependent concept, with its setup hierarchy corrected. This does not establish a general preference for emblems over typography-led designs.
- The hierarchy issue was introduced when mapping the concept into the recipe, not required by the recipe. Two parallel setups should not accidentally signal unequal importance. Either equal visual weight or combining the setup is a possible correction; neither has yet been executed or approved as final artwork.
- “Competing elements” was an imprecise initial diagnosis. Useful judgment should identify the cause and the smallest effective correction while preserving what works.
- The Founder has judged these as proper/good shirts. Customer comprehension, purchase desire, production quality and general repeatability remain untested. Antique/vintage specificity remains a review question, not a demonstrated recognition result.

## Decisions carried into working guidance

Image-native discovery with lightweight attributes; reusable relationships rather than fixed numeric score combinations; garment cut/fabric/color/placement as part of the recipe; two goals of usable execution and taste calibration; semantic jobs of copy/graphics guide selection; first-view comprehension and identity relevance guide review; semantic hierarchy is one of multiple craft judgments.
