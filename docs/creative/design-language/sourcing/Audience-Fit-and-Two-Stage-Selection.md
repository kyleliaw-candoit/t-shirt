# Audience fit and two-stage selection

Decision: Founder agreed October 19, 2026 session. Owner: Creative Director. Applies across identity groups.

## Preserve audience-dependent aesthetic fit

A design-language recipe describes relationships that can be reused, but its desirability depends on the audience, concept, garment and context. Recognizing an identity and wanting to wear its visual treatment are separate judgments. Merely replacing the subject or wording does not establish transfer.

The Founder’s example: SimplyCuteTees’ aesthetics might resonate with some antique/vintage treasure hunters, yet be much less likely to fit Asian-food lovers. Preserve this as a useful fit hypothesis, not a verified population preference or a blanket exclusion. Asian-food interest is not an ethnicity; both identity groups contain multiple tastes. A regional source can be valuable without being universally applicable, and women-oriented marketing is not evidence of one universal feminine aesthetic.

A seller-designated bestseller may sell because of subject matter, visual treatment, the fit between them, brand affinity, garment, price, distribution or other factors. Observing the shirt cannot isolate these causes. Record the possibilities without inventing causal scores.

## Two stages

1. **Discovery/admission:** Is the source commercially signaled and visually useful? Capture the exact evidence, shirt/graphic relationship and original marketing context. Admit useful contrast even when no current target application is obvious. The completed discovery batch contains four initial slots each from seven authorized sources. Selection for breadth is not a sales ranking.
2. **Application selection:** Given a specific identity subgroup, material and concept, does the visual language support comprehension, emotional tone, identity expression and willingness to wear? State the fit hypothesis and evidence, adapt the relationships, inspect the garment result, and preserve feedback. A discovery source is not automatically recommended for every audience.

These are workflow stages, not additional permission gates. Existing candidate/evidence statuses remain separate: observed source context; proposed transfer; application-tested; customer evidence if later available. Never promote internal approval to customer validation.

## Record on each source/family card

- Source audience/positioning and its basis: explicit marketing, visible subject or analyst inference; actual purchaser demographics unknown unless evidenced.
- Visible design and garment relationships, separate from the source’s subject and brand marks.
- Plausible audience/subgroup fit, uncertainty and mismatch conditions; avoid an exhaustive identity-to-style lookup table.
- What must remain for the relationships to work, what can change, and what the new concept must communicate.
- Stage-1 admission reason; stage-2 fit decision for each real application, including unanswered questions.
- Source, hypothesis, execution assessment, Founder feedback and customer evidence kept distinct.

For taste calibration, ask both “Can they understand who this is for?” and “Would this particular audience want to wear this treatment?” Assess those alongside typography, illustration, color, hierarchy, garment integration and wearability. Preserve initial judgments and update reasons after feedback; do not train toward one globally preferred aesthetic.
