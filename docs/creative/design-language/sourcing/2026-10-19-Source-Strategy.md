# Repeatable sourcing for the image-native design-language bank

Research date: October 19, 2026, America/Chicago (October 20 UTC) · Owner: Creative Director · Recommendation for the next discovery batch; 24–30 designs not yet collected

## Recommendation

Use four repeatable routes: **Etsy item-level Bestseller listings, Life is Good's Best Sellers collection, SnorgTees' Best Sellers collection, and HistoreeTees' Current Best Sellers / Greatest Hits collections.** Target 28 distinct visual designs: 12 Etsy, 6 Life is Good, 6 SnorgTees, 4 HistoreeTees. This allocation is a proposed diversity control, not a market-share estimate, a mandatory future quota or a promise that all candidates will qualify.

Keep the evidence classes visible: Etsy's item badge and a brand's own collection designation are different signals. Neither supplies audited design-level sales or proves that the design language caused demand. If only independently verified unit sales qualified, this research would not supply 28 eligible designs. For our discovery purpose, use explicitly labeled commercial proxies already consistent with the Historee pilot.

The next scan should build a reusable source register and visual archive, so a larger pass can revisit the same entry points, identify new designs and resume without redoing the mechanism analysis. Do not select only designs we personally like or only humor shirts.

## Routes that should not supply the core 28 yet

- **Etsy's “Bestselling Tshirts” market page:** currently sorted by Relevancy and includes keyword matches such as author shirts plus custom/digital items. Useful as a discovery route, not a list certified as best sellers. Open individual listings and verify the actual badge.
- **TeePublic `/t-shirts/best-sellers`:** retrieved page shows Relevance sorting and “Best Sellers” as a tag. This is not sufficient evidence of a platform sales ranking. Use only as supplementary visual discovery unless another verifiable item-level commercial signal is found.
- **Threadless Popular:** the shop offers Trending, Popular and New sorts, but this check did not establish that Popular means units sold. Its official historical best-selling articles can supply dated references, but do not demonstrate current winners. Reserve for later visual breadth; do not mix silently into the bestseller quota.
- **6DollarShirts:** category descriptions name examples as best sellers, but a comparable dedicated, verified route was not established in this check. Keep as a possible future source rather than stretching evidence to add a fifth source now.
- Amazon remains shelved, as previously directed. No paid research tool or Kittl dependency is needed for this initial route plan.

## How to collect the next batch

### 1. Separate discovery from admission

Start with the [existing Etsy source index](../../trace/pilots/TRACE-Etsy-120-Evidence.json), preserving source IDs and prior mechanism work. Reopen candidates for images and current commercial evidence. The earlier Etsy report explicitly says screenshots were not archived, so descriptions cannot substitute for a fresh visual capture.

For new Etsy candidates, use fixed neutral queries such as “graphic t shirt”, “nature t shirt”, “hobby t shirt” and “funny t shirt”, with US/English/USD settings recorded. Search results only locate candidates; admission requires the listing badge. Skip promoted positions for sampling; a separate directly verified listing can still qualify. Log query, sort, page/position and admission/rejection reason. Where public search is unavailable, use retained listing URLs or site-restricted search to discover direct listing pages, labeling that route. Do not represent fallback retrieval as a complete Etsy search sweep.

For the three brands, start at the named best-seller collection, record its current default order, and follow its actual pagination or load-more navigation. Open the physical adult T-shirt variant for each candidate. Do not invent pagination parameters or assume a default collection order is a sales rank.

### 2. Require commercial evidence plus usable visual evidence

Each admitted source needs:

- exact product URL, listing/design ID, seller, retrieval date and source route;
- item badge snapshot or collection-membership record, with the signal issuer and wording;
- saved full-shirt image plus the best available detail image; a model view where available, otherwise explicitly unknown wearer relationship;
- selected garment variant/color and listed specifications, separated from visual estimates;
- original image URLs, local archive paths, dimensions and file hashes;
- unresolved issues, source dependencies and inclusion/exclusion reason.

A high-resolution isolated graphic can support lettering/illustration analysis but cannot establish garment placement. If no garment view exists, hold it as a supplementary detail reference rather than count it as a complete-shirt source. Do not generate missing views or upscale a mockup and call the result source evidence.

Exclude blanks, downloads, wholesale/custom-upload listings and designs whose appeal depends on licensed characters or celebrities under our existing scope. A listing-level badge spanning many unrelated selectable designs does not prove which design sells: hold it outside the core batch unless the specific design signal is separable. Color/size variants do not supply independent sales evidence.

**For this visual study, an original graphic-only/no-copy design can qualify.** The earlier mechanism-study exclusion for designs without a decodable mechanism should not erase illustration-led design language from this different assignment. The product must still be a physical shirt with the required commercial/visual evidence.

### 3. Select for coverage after initial screening

Keep a contact sheet of all screened eligible candidates, then select the 28 with enough variety to surface useful relationships. Use a light coverage grid: typography-led / integrated / illustration-led; restrained / medium / expansive footprint; lettering and illustration personality; palette/texture; garment color/cut. These are selection attributes, not predeclared recipe families or sex-based assumptions about taste.

Preserve a mixture of straightforward commercial examples and visually distinctive examples. Record why an eligible candidate was held for repetition or coverage. Favor evergreen designs for most of the batch so October holiday merchandising does not dominate; retain seasonal status rather than implying a representative market sample. If a source cannot supply its target without weakening evidence, report the shortfall and extend that same route before proposing a substitute.

### 4. Deduplicate at the visual-design level

Give a design its own record when visual execution materially differs, even if wording matches another record. Treat recolors, sizes and routine garment variants as linked variants; study useful garment adaptations without inflating distinct-design counts. Identical art sold by multiple stores is one visual design with multiple observed offers, not independent evidence that several creators discovered the recipe. Keep family recurrence, distinct design count, storefront count and commercial evidence separate; do not inherit TRACE mechanism scores as design-language rankings.

### 5. Make the larger pass a continuation

Keep a stable source register containing entry URLs, retrieval method, query/filter/sort settings, pages visited, candidate IDs, duplicate groups and the last completed location. On the next pass, recheck page 1 for changes, then continue past inspected coverage; use item IDs rather than position alone to avoid duplicates when order changes. Append dated observations when badges, collections or images change. Preserve historical captures rather than overwriting them.

Expand within the same four routes according to missing visual capabilities or garment treatments. If one route is exhausted or persistently inaccessible, document that fact before adding a new source. Collection size and popularity labels do not establish how many eligible designs remain.

## Deliverable and checkpoint

Next execution produces 24–30 complete source records, a visual contact sheet, a source/coverage register and admission log. Then group images and develop approximately four to six candidate cards if the visual evidence supports them. The current task researches the acquisition method; it does not claim those records have already been collected or decoded.
