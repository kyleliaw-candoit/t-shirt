# R043 — Material Depth Audit: 60 Candidate Seeds

Research Analyst | 2026-10-22
Companion to [audience research](R043-Everyday-Language-Humor.md).
Purpose: answer the Founder's explicit question, **roughly how many quirky phrases/idioms/slang could supply material for humorous shirts?**

## Answer at the present evidence level

- **Thousands of expressions exist in the wider reference pool.** Oxford's publisher describes more than 10,000 figurative expressions/sayings/proverbs [B1]; Cambridge describes over 7,000 idioms [B2]. These overlap and include unfunny, obscure, regional, dated, or long material. Do not add the totals or call them T-shirt opportunities.
- **This audit supplies 60 distinct candidate seed units**, each linked to an observed language/market source. This is a constructed, purposive inventory, not a random sample or a claim that only 60 exist.
- **12 are the closest-fit editorial candidates** for the original speech-recognition premise; **16 more conversational extensions need stronger context**; **32 are adjacent material** in repetition, figurative language, mishearing and opposed word senses.
- Thus there are **28 mapped conversational seed units and a 60-unit broader development pool in this bounded pass**. They are not 28 or 60 funny, printable, legally cleared designs. Only a creative/usability screen and then buyer evidence can establish that yield.
- It is plausible that a larger search would find substantially more. **No defensible total of commercially usable phrases, or conversion rate from dictionary entry to sellable design, has been established.** A forecast such as “hundreds of winning shirts” would exceed this evidence.

The 12/16/32 distinction is an analyst screen of closeness, not demonstrated audience comprehension or humor. Some single words are merely prompts until a context makes their quirk visible. The 32 adjacent seeds must not silently redefine the audience as all wordplay enthusiasts.

## Counting rules

1. Unit: a phrase/word cluster with a distinct source-language observation, not a layout, garment, color, synonym swap, or design recipe.
2. Yes/no variants occupy one unit. Abbreviations (NGL/TBH) do not add units. Kind/sort-of and could/couldn't-care-less are conservatively paired.
3. Different expressions can share one humorous mechanism. Therefore **60 seeds do not establish 60 mechanisms**, 60 independent customer needs, or 60 repeat purchases.
4. Each row's source establishes existence/use or supply. The “material” interpretation is our inference. Source presence does not establish humor, familiarity, origin, exclusivity, purchase, or clearance.
5. No competitor art or slogans were transformed into supposedly original designs. This is an input inventory, not a concept collection.
6. It is an exact count of this documented list, not an estimate of the English language. There is no statistical extrapolation.

## Screening criteria for eventual usable-material yield

The actual useful count should fall as these filters are applied:

- Speech-habit or taste recognition beyond an isolated vocabulary lesson.
- A clear twist or tension, not a familiar phrase merely reprinted.
- Short/legible enough for apparel, with the intended reading surviving loss of vocal tone.
- Wearer willingness and social comfort, including the fourth attachment source.
- A discernible reason to buy rather than simply share a meme.
- Distinctness from ideas already in the set; provenance and commercial clearance assessed before use.

The closest 12 deserve inspection first as **material**, but this is not commercial prioritization or authorization to design them.

## Implication for the Strategy handoff

Material availability is a **separate depth criterion** from audience size and first-design appeal. The research demonstrates more than one joke and a bounded pool spanning several language behaviors. The hard remaining questions are usable-material yield and repeated buyer interest: how many survive recognition, humor, wearability and originality screens, and whether the same people want several.

Do not promote this pool directly to 60 products. If Strategy recommends further development, evaluate a small varied subset first and record attrition rather than guessing a dictionary-to-design percentage. No market test or creative work begins from this document alone.
