# Umbrella Legal Entity and Consumer Brand Name Guidance

## Purpose

This document defines the working convention for separating the company's umbrella legal entity from its customer-facing brands.

It applies regardless of which LLC name or consumer brand names are ultimately selected.

The intended outcome is:

- customers consistently recognize the brand from which they purchased;
- vendors and partners understand which legal entity owns or operates that brand;
- legal ownership is disclosed truthfully where needed;
- the umbrella entity name does not unintentionally become a customer-facing brand;
- avoidable payment confusion, chargebacks, and naming conflicts are reduced.

This is operational guidance, not individualized legal advice. Requirements should be confirmed against then-current law, platform rules, and professional advice when material.

---

## Core Convention

Use a two-level identity:

| Function | Name used |
|---|---|
| Customer-facing identity | The applicable consumer brand |
| Legal contracting and ownership identity | The umbrella LLC's exact legal name |
| Relationship statement | "[Brand] is operated by [Legal Entity] LLC." |

Generic example:

> **Customer-facing brand:** Example Brand  
> **Legal entity:** Example Holdings LLC  
> **Relationship:** Example Brand is operated by Example Holdings LLC.

The umbrella entity should appear where legal or operational identification is necessary. It should not be presented as an additional consumer brand unless the company deliberately decides to develop it as one.

---

## Why the Separation Matters

A state business-name filing and a trademark perform different functions.

The Texas Secretary of State explains that forming an entity under a legal name does not itself grant the right to use that name in commerce in violation of another party's rights. The USPTO likewise explains that using a business name does not necessarily constitute trademark use, but the name can also function as a trademark when it identifies the source of goods or services.

Mere formation normally presents less trademark risk than market-facing use. Risk can increase when the legal name appears prominently in advertising, product presentation, customer communications, payment descriptors, or other contexts where people may perceive it as identifying the commercial source.

Accordingly, acceptance of an LLC name by Texas is necessary for formation but is not a substitute for reasonable trademark and commercial-use screening.

## 1. Vendors and Partners

### Standard

At the beginning of a formal vendor or partner relationship, identify both the legal entity and the relevant brand:

> **[Legal Entity] LLC, doing business as [Brand]**

After the relationship is clear, routine communication may emphasize the brand. Contracts, tax forms, banking instructions, and other legal or financial records should continue to use the LLC's exact legal name.

### Recommended Usage

| Context | Recommended presentation |
|---|---|
| Contract party | "[Legal Entity] LLC d/b/a [Brand]" |
| W-9 | Exact legal name; business/DBA name where the form requests it |
| Bank and payout account | Exact legal entity name |
| Purchase order | Brand prominently; legal entity in the billing or contracting field |
| Vendor email signature | "[Brand] — a brand operated by [Legal Entity] LLC" |
| Informal abbreviation of LLC | Avoid externally unless intentionally adopted and cleared |

### Assumed-Name Check

Before regularly conducting business under a brand name, confirm whether an assumed-name filing is required or useful under then-current Texas rules. Filing an assumed name records the connection between the brand and the legal entity; it does not itself create trademark ownership or clear conflicts.

## 2. Customer-Facing Payment Descriptors

### Standard

The card-statement descriptor should resemble the brand the customer saw at checkout.

Preferred patterns:

- recognizable version of the brand name;
- recognizable brand plus a short product indicator if needed;
- the same descriptor disclosed at checkout and in the receipt.

Avoid using an unfamiliar umbrella LLC name as the primary customer descriptor when customers purchased from a differently named brand.

### Example

If the brand is "Example Brand":

> **Preferred descriptor:** EXAMPLEBRAND  
> **Avoid:** UNFAMILIAR HOLDING COMPANY NAME

Where space permits, state near checkout or in the confirmation:

> Your card statement will show **EXAMPLEBRAND**.

### Launch Check

Before public launch:

1. Configure the processor's customer-facing descriptor.
2. Complete a small real transaction.
3. Inspect the authorization, settled transaction, receipt, refund, and chargeback-facing name where possible.
4. Record any processor truncation or modification.
5. Align customer support language with the actual descriptor.

The payment processor may still require the LLC's legal name, EIN, banking information, and ownership information internally.

---

## 3. Invoices, Legal Pages, and Return Addresses

### Customer Invoice or Receipt

Use the consumer brand as the primary visual identity and the legal entity as a secondary ownership disclosure.

> **[Brand]**  
> Order #[number]  
>
> Operated by [Legal Entity] LLC

Do not make the legal entity look like a second product or house brand unless that is intentional.

### Terms and Privacy Policy

Identify the relationship clearly near the beginning:

> [Brand] is a brand operated by [Legal Entity] LLC, a Texas limited liability company. In this policy, "[Brand]," "we," "us," and "our" refer to [Legal Entity] LLC.

Adapt the wording to the document and obtain legal review when the company's risk or scale earns it.

### Return Address

Preferred customer-facing pattern:

> [Brand] Returns  
> [Return address]

If the carrier, mailbox provider, fulfillment partner, or other operational requirement calls for the legal entity:

> [Brand] Returns  
> c/o [Legal Entity] LLC  
> [Return address]

Confirm the POD or fulfillment provider's white-label return-address behavior before launch. Do not promise a return destination until the actual fulfillment workflow is verified.

## Operational Guardrails

Unless the company deliberately decides otherwise:

- do not create a consumer-facing logo for the umbrella LLC;
- do not advertise products under the umbrella LLC name;
- do not create public social accounts for the LLC merely because it exists;
- do not use an informal LLC acronym with customers or partners as though it were another brand;
- do not describe the LLC as a venture fund, investment company, advisory firm, or venture studio unless that is factually accurate and legally appropriate;
- do not conceal the LLC where accurate legal ownership disclosure is required;
- do not assume Texas entity-name acceptance resolves trademark or unfair-competition risk.

---

## Brand Launch Checklist

For every consumer brand:

- [ ] Confirm the brand-to-LLC ownership relationship.
- [ ] Complete appropriate name and trademark screening.
- [ ] Confirm assumed-name requirements.
- [ ] Set the contract convention: "[Legal Entity] LLC d/b/a [Brand]."
- [ ] Configure and test the customer-facing payment descriptor.
- [ ] Add the operator disclosure to receipts where appropriate.
- [ ] Add consistent entity identification to terms and privacy materials.
- [ ] Verify the actual return-address workflow.
- [ ] Ensure vendor, banking, tax, and payout records use the correct legal name.
- [ ] Preserve evidence of the configuration and launch checks.
