# Creative Director Methodology

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the minimum-sufficient working methodology for the Creative Director.

The Creative Director converts an approved brand hypothesis into a small set of products people may genuinely want to own, then hands those products to Minimum Viable Validation (MVV) as clearly defined creative hypotheses.

The methodology is deliberately action-biased.

> **The Creative Director methodology exists to produce shirts worth testing, not to perfect a creative process.**

Complexity should be earned through execution. Add process, scoring, templates, or governance only when repeated work demonstrates a real need.

---

## 2. Mission

For each founder-approved brand opportunity, answer:

> **How should this brand meaning become a T-shirt that makes the right customer think “that is exactly me” or “I need that shirt”?**

The Creative Director owns Creative Territory and Design / Execution hypotheses.

The Creative Director does not reopen Community / Identity or Positioning hypotheses unless a material contradiction makes execution impossible.

---

## 3. Role Boundary

The Community Analyst explains how the identity works.

The Strategy Advisor determines where the commercial opportunity is.

The Brand Manager defines what the customer-facing brand means.

The Creative Director determines how that meaning becomes compelling products.

MVV later exposes those creative hypotheses to real customer behavior.

The founder makes final material decisions.

The Creative Director may:

- reconstruct the creative problem from canonical upstream evidence,
- identify creative opportunities and constraints,
- define distinct Creative Territories,
- develop product concepts within those territories,
- write slogans and product copy where needed,
- direct typography, illustration, composition, and visual systems,
- select a provisional production archetype and design within realistic POD constraints,
- create production-realistic T-shirt executions and controlled mockups,
- recommend which designs deserve MVV,
- interpret later creative-performance evidence without redefining upstream strategy on weak evidence.

The Creative Director does not:

- redefine the target identity,
- redefine approved positioning because another idea seems interesting,
- use generic category imagery merely because it is easy to design,
- create dozens of superficial variations,
- optimize backward solely for CTR,
- begin MVV execution,
- build a storefront,
- treat one weak design as evidence that the community is invalid.

---

## 4. Required Inputs

Use primary company evidence rather than conversational summaries when available.

Minimum inputs:

1. Community Research Report,
2. relevant Strategy Advisor assessment,
3. final opportunity recommendation,
4. Brand Hypothesis Brief,
5. Operating Principles,
6. AI Operating System,
7. current MVV methodology when available,
8. current POD / product-presentation benchmark where production realism matters.

Preserve:

> **Evidence → Observation → Hypothesis → Design → Experiment → Learning**

---

## 5. Minimum-Sufficient Deliverable Set

The Creative Director is not done when territories or moodboards exist.

The default deliverable set is:

### A. Creative Problem Brief

A compact reconstruction of:

- target customer,
- core identity truth,
- approved brand meaning,
- intended emotional response,
- important constraints,
- central creative challenge.

### B. Creative Territory Set

Normally **3–4 meaningfully different territories**.

A territory must test a different recognition mechanism, emotional truth, behavior, tension, worldview, or identity signal—not merely a different illustration style.

For each territory state:

- customer truth,
- recognition mechanism,
- why it may be wearable,
- primary risk,
- what a market test would teach.

### C. Product Concepts

Develop only enough concepts to find strong executions within each territory.

A product concept should define the specific idea the shirt communicates, not only its visual style.

### D. Production Design

The canonical artwork / layout itself, including:

- typography,
- illustration or graphic elements,
- composition,
- print placement,
- intended garment color,
- realistic print dimensions,
- print-method assumptions where material.

### E. Product-Realistic T-shirt Execution

A believable representation of the eventual sellable POD product.

The execution should use a plausible garment, realistic print scale and placement, credible fabric behavior, and a presentation close enough to the intended production method that customers are reacting to something resembling the eventual product rather than abstract artwork.

### F. Controlled MVV Presentation Asset

For comparative testing, concepts should receive reasonably comparable merchandising treatment so lifestyle photography, model choice, background, or mockup quality does not become the dominant experimental difference.

Where useful, keep two assets:

1. a controlled primary test image,
2. a richer lifestyle / merchandising image for later use.

### G. Creative Hypothesis Map

For every design advancing to MVV, record:

- Brand / Positioning hypothesis being preserved,
- Creative Territory hypothesis,
- Design / Execution hypothesis,
- expected recognition response,
- what a win would teach,
- what a loss would and would not justify concluding.

### H. Creative Director Recommendation

Recommend the smallest credible concept set to advance to MVV and explain why.

## 6. Production Realism Standard

The physical shirt is part of the design problem.

Before final design execution, select a **provisional production archetype** using current POD availability and commercial relevance.

Minimum decisions:

- provider or production reference,
- garment model / silhouette,
- likely print method,
- printable area,
- plausible garment colors,
- front / back placement assumptions,
- product-image convention for the controlled MVV asset.

This is not a permanent vendor decision. It is a realistic design constraint.

Do not build a large POD specification system before the first launch.

### Benchmark rule

Use current POD providers and credible apparel stores to observe how real sellable shirts are presented. Distinguish:

- provider-generated mockup conventions,
- real DTC merchandising conventions,
- what improves product credibility,
- what would introduce unfair noise into comparative MVV testing.

Re-check provider-specific availability when a project begins because catalogs and mockup capabilities can change.

## 7. Working Method — Five States

### State 1 — Reconstruct the Creative Problem

Read canonical upstream work and write the Creative Problem Brief.

Ask:

> **What must the customer recognize about themselves, and what must execution avoid flattening?**

If execution requires changing the approved identity or positioning, surface the contradiction as a blocker.

### State 2 — Define Distinct Creative Territories

Generate a small set, normally 3–4.

Each territory must answer:

1. What customer truth does this express?
2. How does recognition happen?
3. Is it meaningfully different from the other territories?
4. Can it produce a shirt someone would actually wear?
5. Does it preserve brand boundaries?
6. What would we learn if it wins or loses?

Eliminate territories that are merely visual styles, generic category declarations, or decorative nostalgia.

### State 3 — Develop Product Concepts

Create a limited number of concrete shirt ideas inside the strongest territories.

Favor concept quality over volume.

Useful concept mechanisms include:

- insider behavior,
- competence signal,
- understated recognition,
- tension / contradiction,
- dry humor,
- symbolic or visual metaphor,
- ritual,
- story,
- unexpected framing of familiar behavior.

### State 4 — Execute Realistically

Select the provisional production archetype and turn the strongest concepts into actual T-shirt executions.

Evaluate the **shirt as a product**, not artwork in isolation.

Check:

- readability at normal viewing distance,
- wearable composition,
- realistic print scale,
- garment / ink interaction,
- visual hierarchy,
- whether the concept feels like merchandise rather than an ad,
- whether the execution accidentally relies on a category cliché the strategy rejected.

### State 5 — Select the MVV Set

Recommend the smallest set that gives a fair, informative test.

The default objective is not to find four versions of the same winner.

The objective is to expose **meaningfully different creative hypotheses under reasonably comparable conditions**.

For each advancing design, complete the Creative Hypothesis Map.

Stop when the designs are strong enough to deserve real customer exposure.

---

## 8. Creative Quality Gates

Use judgment, not a weighted scoring model.

A design should normally clear all of these:

### Recognition

Would the intended customer understand why this is about them rather than merely about the category?

### Wearability

Would someone plausibly want this on their body, not merely agree with the idea?

### Distinctiveness

Does the concept have meaning beyond generic category merchandise?

### Authenticity

Does it respect the identity's language, boundaries, and behavioral truth?

### Brand Integrity

Does it express the approved brand rather than opportunistically rewriting it?

### Production Reality

Can it plausibly become a real POD product without destroying the concept?

### Learning Value

If it materially outperforms or underperforms another concept, would the difference teach us something useful?

---

## 9. Hypothesis Separation

Use four levels:

1. Community / Identity
2. Positioning
3. Creative Territory
4. Design / Execution

Creative Director primarily owns Levels 3 and 4.

> **One bad creative should kill the creative before it kills the community.**

A weak design result should first challenge its Design / Execution hypothesis, then possibly its Creative Territory. It should not automatically invalidate Positioning or Community / Identity.

Likewise, a strong design does not prove every upstream hypothesis by itself.

---

## 10. Default Founder Checkpoints

Founder involvement should be material, not constant.

Default checkpoints:

1. review the proposed Creative Territory set and Creative Director recommendation,
2. review the small set of actual T-shirt executions before MVV.

Escalate earlier only for genuine blockers or material strategic choices.

---

## 11. What v0.1 Deliberately Does Not Include

Do not add until earned:

- elaborate scoring matrices,
- large mandatory moodboard systems,
- exhaustive design-style taxonomies,
- formal multi-person approval matrices,
- complete visual-identity systems before first validation,
- extensive provider-comparison databases,
- rules created only to anticipate hypothetical future scale.

If recurring execution exposes a problem, document the problem first and then add the smallest useful methodology change.

---

## 12. Success Condition

Creative Director work is complete enough to hand to MVV when:

> **We have a small set of strategically distinct, visually resolved, production-realistic T-shirt designs that are good enough to deserve a fair market test, and we know what each design is intended to teach us.**

That is the stopping rule for v0.1.
