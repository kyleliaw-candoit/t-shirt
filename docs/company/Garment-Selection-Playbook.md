# Garment Selection Playbook

## 1. Purpose

This playbook defines how the company selects a T-shirt garment or blank without confusing garment quality, audience fit, brand expression, production feasibility, and unit economics.

The governing lesson is:

> **Choose the garment that matches the audience, brand, artwork, and offer—not merely the cheapest or most premium blank.**

Garment selection is important, but reversible. During early validation, choose a credible production archetype, disclose fit honestly, learn from samples and customer evidence, and change the blank when evidence warrants it. Do not delay a useful test while seeking a theoretically perfect garment.

## 2. Evidence standard

This playbook was prompted by a user-supplied transcript of:

- [How to Pick the RIGHT T-Shirt Blank for Your Brand](https://youtu.be/DluQKX0gZHw) (reviewed 2026-09-25)

The video is a useful practitioner source and decision framework. Its product descriptions, customer generalizations, supplier costs, shipping prices, selling prices, and margin illustrations are **source claims**, not automatically company facts.

Before a garment is approved for production, Operations must recheck current:

- manufacturer specifications;
- Printful or selected-provider availability;
- fabric composition by color;
- dimensions and size range;
- print methods and printable area;
- fulfillment regions;
- product, printing, and shipping cost;
- provider warnings and care instructions.

Physical sample evidence and company customer evidence outrank generalized creator claims.

## 3. Role ownership

| Stage | Primary owner | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Audience evidence | Community Analyst | Finds observable fit, weight, style, price-sensitivity, and merchandise preferences |
| Commercial hypothesis | Strategy Advisor | Defines what audience/offer/pricing hypothesis deserves testing |
| Brand requirements | Brand Manager | Defines the physical and emotional qualities the garment should communicate |
| Garment recommendation | Creative Director | Chooses the garment/artwork combination that best expresses the approved brand and design |
| Operational feasibility | Operations Manager | Validates supplier, catalog, variants, colors, print compatibility, shipping, disclosures, and maintainability |
| Unit economics | Finance Analyst | Calculates contribution using verified costs and preserves assumptions versus actuals |
| Material approval | Founder | Approves material product, sample, spending, and launch decisions when required |
| Durable knowledge | Knowledge Librarian | Preserves the playbook, decision record, and validated lessons |

No single role chooses the garment in isolation.

The decision chain is:

> **Audience evidence → brand requirements → Creative recommendation → Operations feasibility → Finance economics → Strategy test implications → Founder approval where material**

A role may block a candidate only within its domain:

- Brand Manager: brand mismatch;
- Creative Director: weak product or artwork/garment mismatch;
- Operations Manager: unavailable, unreliable, or unproducible;
- Finance Analyst: economics misstated or outside an approved boundary;
- Strategy Advisor: candidate does not support the approved commercial test;
- Founder: final material business decision.

## 4. Selection criteria

Evaluate candidates across five lenses.

### 4.1 Audience and fit

- intended customer mix and body-fit expectations;
- fitted, standard, relaxed, boxy, oversized, long, or short silhouette;
- garment dimensions rather than vague “true to size” language;
- size inclusivity and whether larger sizes change the intended fit;
- tolerance for heavyweight, lightweight, fitted, or sheer garments;
- likely use context: everyday wear, active use, work, event, gift, or fashion.

Audience demographic generalizations are hypotheses, not rules. Validate them through community evidence, samples, returns, reviews, and customer behavior.

### 4.2 Brand and perceived value

- whether the garment feels accessible, substantial, refined, vintage, fashion-oriented, utilitarian, or premium;
- whether garment color and finish support the brand world;
- whether the customer is likely to recognize or seek the blank by name;
- whether the intended selling price matches perceived quality;
- whether batch variation or garment-dyed character is a benefit or a liability.

“Premium” is not an absolute ranking. A heavyweight garment can feel higher quality to one audience and uncomfortable to another.

### 4.3 Artwork and print compatibility

- knit and surface smoothness;
- fine-line, small-text, halftone, texture, and color requirements;
- fabric composition by color, especially heathers and blends;
- garment color interaction with ink and underbase;
- printable area and placement;
- shrinkage or distortion risk;
- whether muted garment colors support or fight the artwork;
- provider-specific DTG, DTF, embroidery, or other print limitations.

### 4.4 Operations and customer expectations

- provider availability and fulfillment coverage;
- sizes and colors that can be kept consistent;
- cost and shipping by destination;
- synchronization and substitution risk;
- care instructions;
- batch variation;
- real measurements and size-chart quality;
- sample and real-model photography;
- clear disclosures for slim fit, shrinkage, garment dye, bleeding, color variation, or transparency.

Many apparent “quality problems” are expectation mismatches. Accurate merchandising can reduce avoidable returns without hiding tradeoffs.

### 4.5 Economics

Use verified provider data in the [Shopify and Meta Unit Economics Planning Model](../finance/Shopify-Meta-Unit-Economics-Planning-Model.md).

At minimum include:

- blank plus printing;
- additional print placements;
- fulfillment shipping absorbed by the seller;
- payment-processing fees;
- discounts and shipping revenue;
- return/replacement reserve;
- Shopify and app costs at the appropriate level;
- advertising cost when evaluating contribution after acquisition.

Do not call selling price minus production and shipping “profit.” Use the company's defined contribution metrics.

## 7. Standard decision process

### Step 1 — Define requirements

Brand Manager and Creative Director state:

- target wearer and use context;
- intended silhouette, weight, hand feel, and visual character;
- artwork and color requirements;
- intended price position;
- non-negotiable brand or customer-experience boundaries.

### Step 2 — Shortlist candidates

Creative Director recommends a small candidate set. Avoid exhaustive blank research unless the shortlist cannot meet requirements.

### Step 3 — Validate operational facts

Operations checks the actual Printful or selected-provider catalog and records retrieval date, product IDs, variants, sizes, colors, composition, print method, costs, shipping, fulfillment coverage, and warnings.

### Step 4 — Model economics

Finance calculates pre-ad contribution and relevant scenarios using current verified inputs. Strategy determines whether the result supports the commercial hypothesis.

### Step 5 — Inspect samples

Order samples only with required Founder spending authorization. Inspect:

- fit across relevant body types and sizes;
- fabric hand, weight, opacity, seams, and collar;
- print sharpness, scale, color, and hand feel;
- shrinkage and distortion after care-compliant washing;
- dye transfer, fading, and batch variation where applicable;
- packaging and delivered condition.

### Step 6 — Approve and document

Record the decision, rejected alternatives, reasons, disclosures, current costs, sample evidence, and switching triggers. Maintain stable company product and design IDs even if a garment changes; record the garment/version change explicitly.

### Step 7 — Learn and revise

Monitor fit complaints, returns, replacements, reviews, customer-service contacts, and repeat purchase. Distinguish:

- garment defect;
- print defect;
- expectation mismatch;
- sizing/merchandising failure;
- audience/brand mismatch.

Switching blanks is allowed when evidence warrants it. Do not disguise a garment change inside an unrelated listing update.

## 8. Product-page minimums

For each garment, provide:

- actual garment measurements by size;
- model measurements, worn size, and real-person photography when feasible;
- fit description in concrete terms;
- fiber composition and material exceptions by color;
- care instructions;
- size-up or size-down guidance when evidence supports it;
- garment-dye, bleeding, batch-variation, shrinkage, or opacity disclosures where relevant;
- shipping and made-to-order expectations;
- a clear return/replacement policy approved under company governance.

AI mockups may support presentation, but they should not be the only evidence customers receive about fit.

## 10. Worth the Detour application

For Worth the Detour, **Comfort Colors 1717 deserves evaluation** because its garment-dyed, lived-in, heavyweight character may support the antiques/vintage brand world.

This is a hypothesis, not an approved garment decision. The evaluation must check:

- whether the muted garment palette supports the approved artwork;
- whether the target audience values the heavier relaxed feel;
- actual Printful availability and cost;
- fit and size range;
- batch/color behavior;
- print fidelity;
- physical sample evidence;
- whether the resulting price supports the validation and contribution model.

Gildan 64000 remains a plausible lower-risk comparator if the objective is a broadly acceptable soft unisex shirt before garment preference is known.

## 11. Knowledge update policy

Update this playbook only when repeated evidence changes the general method. Record product-specific facts and decisions in Garment Decision Records rather than turning this document into a catalog snapshot.

Preserve:

- source claims as source claims;
- provider facts with retrieval dates;
- financial assumptions separately from actuals;
- sample observations separately from customer behavior;
- decisions and switching triggers explicitly.
