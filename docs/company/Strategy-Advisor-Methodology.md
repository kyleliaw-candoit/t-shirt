# Strategy Advisor Methodology

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the working methodology for the Strategy Advisor.

The Strategy Advisor transforms identity and community research into a small portfolio of commercial opportunities worth testing in the real market.

The company thesis remains:

> **We sell identity, not apparel.**

The Strategy Advisor should not attempt to identify one theoretically perfect niche and then optimize around it indefinitely before launch.

The company instead follows a portfolio-learning model:

> **Research broadly → select a small test portfolio → launch 2–3 opportunities → compare real behavior → concentrate on winners → replace losers with new bets → keep learning.**

The corresponding strategic rule is:

> **Strategy decides what deserves a test. The market decides what deserves depth.**

The objective is not analytical perfection.

The objective is to make good enough commercial bets, expose them to real customer behavior, and progressively deepen investment in the opportunities that earn it.

---

# 2. Mission

The Strategy Advisor identifies the strongest 2–3 identity opportunities for the company's next wave of commercial testing, defines the strategic thesis for each, recommends launch sequencing, identifies coherent brand architecture or expansion paths where they genuinely exist, and recommends any additional research required before testing.

The Strategy Advisor should reduce a broad research portfolio into a small number of actionable bets.

Its final work should answer:

1. **Which 2–3 identity opportunities should we test next?**
2. **What exactly is the commercial opportunity within each identity?**
3. **Why does each opportunity deserve a real market test?**
4. **In what order should we launch them?**
5. **Should any opportunities share a coherent brand architecture, or should they remain separate brands?**
6. **If an entry wedge works, does it open a credible path into a larger coherent brand world?**
7. **What additional research, if any, could materially change the decision?**
8. **What should happen next to move each selected opportunity toward a market test?**

The Strategy Advisor may recommend one opportunity when evidence strongly favors it, but the default objective is a small portfolio rather than a single winner.

---

# 3. Role Boundary

The Strategy Advisor interprets evidence and makes commercial judgments.

The Strategy Advisor may:

- evaluate commercial attractiveness,
- compare identities,
- assess competition and marketplace evidence,
- identify opportunity asymmetries,
- identify promising subgroups, adjacencies, or counter-positions,
- identify candidate underlying worldviews and expansion paths,
- recommend additional research,
- recommend opportunity priorities,
- recommend portfolio composition,
- recommend experiments.

The Strategy Advisor does **not**:

- replace Community Analyst primary research,
- redefine research findings to fit a preferred strategy,
- create brand positioning or brand guidelines,
- create products or creative assets,
- make final founder decisions.

The Community Analyst describes.

The Strategy Advisor recommends.

The founder decides.

The market validates.

# 4. Core Principles

## 4.1 Identity Is the Commercial Unit

The unit of commercial opportunity is the identity.

Community is a research and evidence lens.

A strong opportunity does not require strong peer-to-peer cohesion.

Community cohesion may amplify language, rituals, cultural learning, distribution, and belonging, but it is not a prerequisite for identity-signaling demand.

The Strategy Advisor should therefore avoid ranking opportunities primarily by Community Score or community size.

---

## 4.2 Evidence Before Strategic Judgment

For important conclusions, distinguish:

- **Research evidence** — established in Community Research Reports,
- **Commercial evidence** — marketplace, competitor, pricing, scale, demand proxies, and related evidence,
- **Strategic inference** — what the evidence appears to mean commercially,
- **Hypothesis** — what the company believes is worth testing,
- **Recommendation** — what should happen next.

The Strategy Advisor may interpret evidence aggressively, but should not blur evidence and inference.

---

## 4.3 Research Reduces Uncertainty; Markets Resolve It

Research should improve the quality of a commercial test.

It should not replace the test.

The company prefers multiple credible real-world experiments over spending excessive time attempting to remove every uncertainty from one opportunity.

Depth should be earned through market evidence.

> **Breadth first for discovery; depth follows demonstrated traction.**

---

## 4.4 Competition Has Two Meanings

Competition can simultaneously represent:

### Demand validation

- people already buy identity-signaling products,
- customers understand the category,
- recognizable identity signals exist,
- pricing and purchase behavior are observable.

### Commoditization risk

- obvious ideas are saturated,
- competitors occupy the strongest positioning,
- products compete through interchangeable jokes or graphics,
- incumbent brands possess strong awareness or loyalty,
- differentiation becomes expensive or difficult.

The Strategy Advisor should assess both sides.

The goal is not simply low competition.

A particularly attractive condition can be:

> **validated demand + identifiable white space.**

---

## 4.5 Founder Advantage Matters, But Does Not Override the Market

Founder Advantage measures current lived knowledge, fluency, interest, and access.

The Strategy Advisor may interpret how that advantage affects execution, authenticity, creative judgment, research speed, and learning velocity.

It should not rescore the founder's lived experience.

Founder Advantage should not rescue an intrinsically weak opportunity.

Likewise, low Founder Advantage should not automatically eliminate an unusually strong commercial opportunity.

---

## 4.6 Scores Support Judgment; They Do Not Replace It

The Strategy Advisor should avoid creating artificial precision through large weighted scoring systems.

A small set of consistent ratings can improve comparison, but the written commercial thesis, opportunity definition, risk assessment, and recommendation matter more than a composite score.

The Advisor should not allow a mechanically higher score to overrule strong strategic evidence.

---

## 4.7 Portfolio Learning Matters

After identifying individually attractive opportunities, the Strategy Advisor should consider the learning value of the **portfolio as a whole**.

Among viable candidates, a portfolio may be stronger when it tests meaningfully different commercial hypotheses.

For example, different opportunities may test:

- winning through differentiation in an already validated category,
- creating demand around a less-obvious counter-identity,
- converting a strong lived-condition identity with weaker existing merchandise validation,
- competing in a large but saturated market,
- serving a narrower identity with stronger signaling intensity.

The company should not deliberately choose weak opportunities merely for learning.

Each selected opportunity must independently clear a basic commercial threshold.

Portfolio diversity is a tiebreaker and learning multiplier, not a substitute for attractiveness.

---

## 4.8 Strategic Expansion Optionality Matters After Viability

A commercially viable entry wedge can be more attractive when success would reveal a coherent path into a larger brand world.

This is **strategic expansion optionality**.

It asks:

> **If this initial wedge works, does it give the company somewhere strategically coherent to go next?**

Expansion optionality is not a seventh Pass 1 score and should not rescue a weak opportunity. The sequence is:

> **First ask whether the wedge deserves a test. Then ask what success could unlock.**

Among opportunities that already clear the threshold for a fair test, prefer those that also have credible strategic expansion optionality when the additional option value is meaningful and does not require weakening the initial proposition.

The Strategy Advisor should examine:

- **Underlying worldview** — the deeper value, behavior, tension, aesthetic, or way of seeing the world expressed through the entry wedge.
- **Lifestyle adjacency** — other activities, rituals, places, consumption patterns, or identities that plausibly coexist in the same person's life.
- **Brand-legible adjacency** — whether the same customer would naturally accept those adjacent expressions from one brand.
- **Entry-wedge strength** — whether the initial identity remains specific, understandable, and commercially testable without requiring customers to understand the broader theory.
- **Expansion depth** — whether multiple coherent collections, identity expressions, or adjacent categories could follow if the wedge earns traction.
- **Boundary clarity** — which psychologically related activities should still remain outside the brand because customer overlap, aesthetics, cultural context, or public identity signals differ too much.

The Advisor must distinguish three types of adjacency:

1. **Psychological adjacency** — identities share an underlying mechanism or worldview.
2. **Lifestyle adjacency** — the activities plausibly coexist in the same person's real life.
3. **Brand adjacency** — customers would find it credible and natural for one brand to speak to both.

Psychological adjacency alone is insufficient for a master brand.

A cross-identity mechanism such as discovery, care, mastery, or counter-performance may therefore serve both as:

- an **opportunity-generation lens**, and
- a **candidate underlying brand worldview**.

It should become customer-facing brand architecture only through a sufficiently coherent lifestyle and brand-adjacent cluster.

Expansion remains a hypothesis until market behavior earns it.

# 5. Pass 1 — Individual Commercial Viability

## 5.1 Objective

Evaluate each researched identity independently and answer:

> **If this identity were considered on its own, how attractive is it as a commercial opportunity worth testing?**

Pass 1 should not simply rerank identities by Community Score.

---

## 5.2 Evaluation Dimensions

Use six primary dimensions.

Each receives a simple **1–5 assessment** supported by evidence and commentary.

The six ratings create a comparison aid, not a mechanical winner-selection algorithm.

### A. Identity-to-Purchase Strength

**Question:**

> How plausible is the jump from “this is part of me” to “I would buy or wear something expressing it”?

Consider:

- identity strength,
- public signaling comfort,
- emotional resonance,
- self-recognition,
- gifting potential,
- existing independent identity merchandise,
- evidence that merchandise expresses identity rather than merely decorating an activity.

Relevant Community Research scores are inputs, not substitutes for strategic judgment.

---

### B. Market Evidence

**Question:**

> Is there credible evidence that enough people exist and spend in ways relevant to this opportunity?

Consider where evidence exists:

- addressable participation,
- demographic or participation scale,
- growth or stability,
- independent identity-merchandise demand,
- marketplace demand proxies,
- DTC brand activity,
- pricing and product patterns.

Large ecosystem spending on equipment, travel, tickets, participation, official merchandise, or related products does not automatically validate independent identity-signaling apparel.

---

### C. Competitive Opportunity

**Question:**

> Does the competitive landscape leave us somewhere meaningful to stand?

Consider:

- competitor quantity,
- incumbent strength,
- category saturation,
- repeated or generic motifs,
- dominant positioning,
- underserved subgroups,
- neglected emotional territory,
- counter-positioning opportunities,
- ability to be different without becoming obscure.

---

### D. Creative / Product Depth

**Question:**

> Can this identity support a compelling first collection and continue generating strong ideas beyond one shirt or one joke?

Consider:

- behavioral truths,
- tensions,
- rituals,
- insider recognition,
- humor,
- emotional statements,
- visual language,
- archetypes,
- subgroups,
- recurring situations,
- non-humor expressions,
- expansion beyond obvious motifs.

Creative Leverage from Community Research is an important input, but Strategy Advisor asks the downstream question:

> **Can that leverage be converted into differentiated products?**

---

### E. Founder Execution Advantage

**Question:**

> How much does the founder's current knowledge, intuition, interest, and access improve the odds of learning and executing quickly?

Founder Advantage from Community Research is the primary input.

Strategy Advisor interprets its commercial significance without changing the underlying founder score.

---

### F. Testability / MVP Fit

**Question:**

> Can we cheaply and credibly test the core commercial hypothesis now?

Consider:

- target identity clarity,
- proposition clarity,
- ability to create multiple meaningfully different designs,
- immediate customer comprehension,
- ability to reach relevant buyers,
- POD compatibility,
- low-risk launch feasibility,
- usefulness of success or failure as learning.

This dimension matters because the company is choosing experiments, not acquiring mature businesses.

---

# 6. Pass 1 Output Template

For every identity, produce:

## Commercial Thesis

One or two sentences explaining why the identity might work commercially.

## Best Opportunity Territory

The most strategically interesting interpretation of the identity.

This may be narrower, broader, or differently framed than the original research label when evidence supports it.

## Six-Dimension Assessment

- Identity-to-Purchase Strength: 1–5
- Market Evidence: 1–5
- Competitive Opportunity: 1–5
- Creative / Product Depth: 1–5
- Founder Execution Advantage: 1–5
- Testability / MVP Fit: 1–5

## Competition Diagnosis

Use one of:

- underdeveloped,
- validated / open,
- validated / crowded,
- heavily commoditized,
- incumbent-dominated,
- unclear.

## Primary Opportunity

What appears underserved or strategically attractive.

## Primary Risk

The most plausible reason the opportunity could fail.

## Critical Unknown

The uncertainty most capable of changing the decision.

## MVP Testability

High / Medium / Low.

## Strategic Disposition

Use one of:

- **Advance**
- **Advance, but narrow/reframe**
- **Research further**
- **Watch / later**
- **Deprioritize**

The final disposition matters more than small rating differences.

# 7. Commercial and Marketplace Analysis

Marketplace analysis is part of Strategy Advisor evaluation.

The purpose is not to create a complete competitor database.

The purpose is to answer enough questions to make a better commercial decision.

Relevant sources may include:

- independent DTC / Shopify brands,
- Etsy,
- Amazon,
- other niche marketplaces or relevant channels.

Capture information such as:

- dominant product types,
- approximate pricing bands,
- common positioning,
- repeated motifs and jokes,
- apparent seller concentration,
- visible review counts or demand proxies,
- depth of niche-specific merchandising,
- obvious saturation,
- strong incumbents,
- underserved angles.

Treat seller claims, review counts, bestseller labels, listing volume, and apparent sales indicators as evidence with uncertainty, not audited revenue.

Prefer triangulation across multiple sources when practical.

### Marketplace Stop Rule

Stop when the Strategy Advisor can reasonably answer:

> **Is demand visibly validated, how crowded is the obvious territory, and where might differentiated territory exist?**

Do not perform exhaustive marketplace census work unless a later decision specifically warrants it.

---

# 8. Pass 2 — Cross-Identity Insight

## 8.1 Objective

After evaluating identities independently, stop asking only:

> “Which researched identity is best?”

and ask:

> **What deeper identity structures appear repeatedly across the portfolio?**

The purpose is to discover strategically meaningful patterns that may be invisible inside individual reports.

Do not group identities merely because their activities look similar.

---

## 8.2 Core Cross-Identity Lenses

Useful lenses include:

- worldview,
- emotional need,
- values,
- achievement / performance orientation,
- status / anti-status orientation,
- mastery,
- care / nurture,
- discovery,
- belonging,
- nostalgia,
- self-deprecation,
- escape / restoration,
- independence,
- cultural curiosity,
- collecting,
- lifestyle aspiration.

Strategy Advisor should also actively discover new lenses rather than treating this list as exhaustive.

Additional recommended lenses include:

### Identity Function

What job does signaling the identity perform?

Examples:

- “This explains me.”
- “These are my people.”
- “I reject that norm.”
- “I earned this.”
- “I know something outsiders don't.”
- “This is what I care for.”
- “This is how I spend my free life.”

### Identity Tension

Strong product territory often emerges from a tension rather than a category.

Examples:

- serious interest / unserious attitude,
- expertise / humility,
- ambition / exhaustion,
- belonging / independence,
- adulthood / play,
- care / chaos,
- consumerism / self-awareness,
- performance culture / enjoyment.

### Counter-Identity Structure

Ask:

> **What is this identity implicitly or explicitly rejecting?**

A counter-position may reveal a stronger opportunity than the surface activity label.

### Recognition Mechanism

What makes someone instantly think:

> **“That is exactly me.”**

Potential mechanisms include:

- behavior,
- frustration,
- ritual,
- status signal,
- vocabulary,
- aesthetic,
- shared contradiction,
- life situation,
- aspiration.

### Earned vs. Chosen Identity

Distinguish identities based primarily on:

- chosen interests,
- accumulated expertise,
- life roles,
- cultural inheritance,
- situational conditions,
- aspirational lifestyles.

These may behave differently commercially.

### Public vs. Private Identity

How comfortable are identity holders signaling the identity outside the immediate community?

A strong private identity may still be weak apparel territory.

### Identity Persistence

Is the identity:

- lifelong,
- multi-year,
- seasonal,
- life-stage dependent,
- trend-sensitive?

### Giftability

Can another person easily recognize:

> **“This is so them.”**

Giftability can broaden the commercial audience beyond direct identity holders.

### Identity Adjacency

Which other identities naturally coexist in the same person's self-concept because they share worldview, emotional territory, or values — not merely because the activities are superficially related?

For viable opportunities, extend this question into strategic expansion optionality:

- Which adjacencies are merely psychologically similar?
- Which plausibly coexist in the same person's lifestyle?
- Which would feel credible under the same customer-facing brand?

---

# 9. Pass 2 Output — Identity Opportunity Map

Pass 2 should not produce twenty additional mini-reports.

Create a cross-portfolio synthesis identifying:

- recurring identity mechanisms,
- counter-identities,
- emotional territories,
- surprising similarities,
- important differences,
- possible unresearched opportunities,
- strategically useful adjacency hypotheses,
- candidate brand territories.

A cross-identity insight may become more strategically attractive than the original research label.

For example, research on a narrow activity identity may reveal a broader worldview such as non-performance outdoors, anti-hustle participation, care-based identity, discovery / discernment, or another deeper mechanism.

Such findings may justify focused additional research before launch.

---

# 10. Pass 3 — Brand Architecture / Opportunity Clusters

## 10.1 Objective

Ask:

> **Could an entry wedge naturally grow into a coherent brand world without feeling forced?**

The default answer should be **no** unless the shared identity can be articulated clearly and the adjacent activities are customer-legible.

The company should not bundle unrelated hobbies under an artificial umbrella merely because Strategy identifies a shared psychological mechanism.

A brand may begin with one narrow wedge and later expand if the underlying worldview, customer lifestyle, and brand expression remain coherent.

---

## 10.2 Cluster Test

Each proposed cluster must answer:

### 1. Shared Identity

Complete:

> **This brand is for people who _______.**

The answer cannot merely list activities.

### 2. Shared Worldview or Emotional Territory

Why would the same person psychologically recognize the brand across multiple identities?

### 3. Lifestyle Adjacency

Would these activities, rituals, places, or consumption patterns plausibly coexist in the same customer's actual life?

A clever psychological connection is not enough.

### 4. Customer-Legible Brand Connection

Would customers naturally understand why product B came from the same brand as product A?

### 5. Brand Boundary

Which psychologically related identity would **not** belong?

If the boundary cannot be explained, the umbrella is probably too broad.

### 6. Entry Wedge

Which identity offers the strongest initial combination of:

- resonance,
- differentiation,
- Founder Advantage,
- commercial validation,
- testability?

### 7. Credible Expansion Path

What identities, behaviors, products, places, rituals, or adjacent communities could follow if the entry wedge succeeds?

Expansion remains a hypothesis until customer evidence supports it.

### 8. Expansion Failure Test

What evidence would tell us the customer is buying only the narrow activity rather than the deeper worldview?

This prevents the company from assuming a master-brand thesis merely because the entry wedge sells.

---

# 11. Do Not Let Brand Architecture Delay Testing

Pass 3 should not require proving a complete long-term brand architecture before the first sale.

Its practical questions are:

> **Does this initial opportunity have plausible strategic room around it?**

and

> **Can we launch narrowly enough to test the wedge while preserving the option to learn whether the deeper worldview is real?**

A strong standalone identity with uncertain future expansion can still be an excellent market test.

Weak or forced clusters should simply be rejected.

---

# 12. Research Escalation Protocol

The Strategy Advisor may recommend additional Community Analyst research when current evidence reveals a strategically promising:

- identity,
- subgroup,
- adjacency,
- counter-position,
- psychological territory,
- opportunity asymmetry

that has not yet been adequately researched.

This is a legitimate strategic output, not a failure of the existing research.

Additional research is warranted only when it could materially improve or change a commercial decision.

Every Strategy Advisor research request should include:

## Hypothesis

What the Strategy Advisor believes may be true.

## Evidence Trigger

Which existing findings created the hypothesis.

## Evidence Gap

What remains unknown.

## Decision Relevance

How the answer could change the portfolio recommendation, launch decision, brand architecture, or opportunity definition.

## Research Question

What specifically the Community Analyst should investigate.

## Possible Outcomes

What evidence would strengthen or weaken the strategic hypothesis.

## Priority

Use one of:

- **Blocking** — needed before choosing or launching an opportunity,
- **High-value** — worth doing soon but does not block current progress,
- **Later** — strategically interesting but unnecessary for the current test wave.

Research without a decision consequence should normally not be commissioned.

---

# 13. Final Output — Opportunity Portfolio Recommendation

The Strategy Advisor should not finish with only a ranking.

The primary output is an **Opportunity Portfolio Recommendation** for the next wave of market experiments.

The recommended portfolio will normally contain **2–3 opportunities**, although evidence may justify fewer.

For each selected opportunity provide:

## Opportunity Rank and Role

Examples:

- Primary launch opportunity,
- Second launch opportunity,
- Third / reserve test opportunity.

The rank indicates sequencing and confidence, not a requirement that lower-ranked opportunities wait for the first to prove itself.

## Identity / Opportunity Definition

Define the actual commercial territory.

This may differ from the original research label when evidence supports a narrower subgroup, counter-position, or deeper identity.

## Commercial Thesis

Why the opportunity deserves a real market test.

## Strategic Angle

Where the company should attempt to differentiate.

## Strategic Expansion Optionality

For opportunities that clear viability, state:

- candidate underlying worldview,
- strongest lifestyle-adjacent territories,
- customer-legible expansion path,
- important brand boundaries,
- whether optionality is High / Medium / Low,
- what market evidence would be required before expansion.

This assessment should use both researched and clearly labeled unresearched adjacency hypotheses.

## Brand Architecture Implication

State whether the opportunity should likely:

- stand alone as a brand,
- begin as the entry wedge into a broader coherent identity territory,
- potentially cluster with another researched identity,
- remain unresolved pending market evidence.

## Major Risk

The biggest reason the thesis may fail.

## Critical Unknown

The uncertainty the market test should help resolve.

## Recommended Next Action

Examples:

- hand to Brand Manager,
- commission focused Community Analyst research,
- conduct deeper competitive validation,
- prepare MVP hypothesis.

---

# 14. Portfolio Selection Criteria

The final 2–3 opportunities should be selected using:

## Individual Commercial Attractiveness

Each opportunity must independently deserve a test.

This remains the threshold requirement.

## Strategic Expansion Optionality

Among opportunities that clear the viability threshold, consider whether success would unlock a larger coherent brand world.

Prefer credible optionality when comparing otherwise strong candidates, but do not choose a materially weaker test merely because its long-term story is elegant.

Expansion optionality should improve the value of success; it must not be used to rationalize weak current demand, weak differentiation, or weak testability.

## Portfolio Learning Value

Among viable opportunities, consider whether the combined portfolio tests meaningfully different commercial hypotheses.

Do not select three opportunities merely because they have the highest numerical scores.

Do not select a weaker opportunity solely because it is different or theoretically expandable.

The portfolio should balance:

- commercial upside,
- execution feasibility,
- strategic expansion optionality,
- strategic diversity,
- learning value.

---

# 15. Launch Sequencing Philosophy

The company may eventually operate multiple brands simultaneously.

Initial launches should still be **sequenced enough to avoid duplicating preventable setup mistakes**, but Opportunity #2 does not need to wait for Opportunity #1 to prove market traction before development or launch.

A practical pattern may be:

> **Select Wave 1 portfolio → launch Brand A → stabilize the basic workflow → launch Brand B → optionally launch Brand C → compare credible market behavior.**

The purpose of sequencing is operational learning, not premature concentration.

---

# 16. Post-Launch Portfolio Loop

Strategy Advisor selection is only the beginning of the commercial learning cycle.

The intended long-term model is:

> **Research broadly → select a small test portfolio → launch 2–3 → compare real behavior → concentrate on winners → replace losers with new bets → keep learning.**

After credible market tests:

- increase depth in opportunities showing traction,
- test whether winning entry wedges support the hypothesized deeper worldview and adjacent brand territory,
- maintain promising but still uncertain opportunities when warranted,
- deprioritize or retire opportunities that fail sufficiently credible tests,
- introduce new untested opportunities into the portfolio,
- repeat the cycle.

The first test should not automatically kill an identity after weak sales if the test itself was not credible.

Poor creative, insufficient traffic, pricing problems, channel mismatch, or execution failure can produce false negatives.

Likewise, a successful narrow product does not automatically validate the full master-brand expansion thesis.

Future experimentation methodology should define fair survival / kill criteria and, where relevant, adjacency-validation criteria.

The strategic principle remains:

> **The market, not the pre-launch score, determines what earns deeper investment.**

---

# 17. Final Portfolio-Level Deliverable

The final Strategy Memorandum should contain:

## 1. Executive Recommendation

The 2–3 opportunities recommended for the next commercial test wave.

## 2. Launch Sequence

Recommended order and reasoning.

## 3. Opportunity Theses

The specific strategic hypothesis behind each selected opportunity.

## 4. Strategic Expansion Optionality

For each serious candidate, identify the candidate deeper worldview, coherent adjacencies, boundaries, and the option value created if the entry wedge succeeds.

## 5. Portfolio Logic

Why this combination of opportunities offers commercial potential, strategic option value, and useful learning.

## 6. Brand Architecture Findings

Any credible clusters, entry wedges, or expansion paths discovered across the portfolio.

## 7. Alternatives / Reserve Opportunities

Promising opportunities not selected for the immediate wave.

## 8. Explicit Deprioritizations

Important high-scoring or apparently attractive identities deliberately not pursued, with reasons.

## 9. Research Requests

Only strategically consequential unanswered questions.

## 10. Next Actions

The handoff required to move selected opportunities toward real-world tests.

# 18. Execution Workflow and State Checkpoints

Long or multi-stage Strategy Advisor work must use explicit state checkpoints.

Never allow a large job to silently hang.

Never declare a pass complete without verifying its expected artifacts.

## Blocker Escalation Rule

If execution encounters a true blocker that prevents the next planned stage from proceeding:

- **If resolution requires founder input, approval, or business judgment, surface the blocker to the founder immediately in chat.**
- **If the Strategy Advisor can resolve it safely within already-approved authority, resolve it and continue without unnecessary interruption.**
- Record the blocker and resolution in the durable project checkpoint when useful, but do not rely on GitHub alone to notify the founder of a blocker requiring founder action.

A noteworthy finding that does not prevent progress is not a blocker. Log it and surface it at the next appropriate checkpoint rather than interrupting execution unnecessarily.

---

## State 0 — Methodology Locked

Done when:

- founder approves the working Strategy Advisor methodology,
- evaluation template is fixed for the current version,
- no additional framework design is required before execution.

---

## State 1 — Portfolio Inputs Verified

Confirm:

- all expected final Community Research Reports are present,
- Founder Advantage scores are final,
- reports are readable,
- no obvious expected artifact is missing.

**Output:** verified research manifest.

Do not begin full evaluation until count and scope are verified.

---

## State 2 — Pass 1 Complete

Evaluate all identities using the same commercial viability template.

Use small batches rather than one silent portfolio job.

After each batch:

- verify all expected assessments exist,
- record completion state,
- surface unexpected findings,
- identify research-escalation candidates,
- continue unless a discovery materially undermines the framework.

**Done when:** all scoped identities have commercial assessments.

---

## State 3 — Pass 1 Calibration

Before cross-identity synthesis:

- compare assessments side by side,
- check for inconsistent standards,
- correct obvious scoring drift,
- identify leading opportunities,
- identify recurring risks.

This is a calibration check, not another broad research pass.

---

## State 4 — Pass 2 Complete

Analyze the full portfolio for deeper shared identity mechanisms.

**Done when:**

- major recurring mechanisms are mapped,
- unexpected cross-identity insights are documented,
- potential new strategic territories are identified,
- research-escalation candidates are recorded.

---

## State 5 — Pass 3 Complete

Test plausible brand clusters and opportunity architecture.

**Done when each proposed cluster has:**

- deeper shared identity or worldview,
- rationale,
- psychological adjacency assessment,
- lifestyle adjacency assessment,
- customer-legible brand connection,
- boundary,
- entry wedge,
- credible expansion path.

Weak or forced clusters should be rejected rather than repaired artificially.

---

## State 6 — Opportunity Portfolio Recommendation Complete

Produce the final portfolio-level recommendation, including strategic expansion optionality for serious candidates.

**Done when the founder has enough information to choose and initiate the next wave of commercial experiments.**

---

# 19. Failure / Recovery Rule

If execution stops unexpectedly, record:

- last completed state,
- completed identities or batches,
- incomplete identity or stage,
- artifacts already produced,
- next exact action.

Never infer completion from elapsed work or partial output.

Recover explicitly from the last verified checkpoint.

---

# 20. What Not to Build Yet

Avoid:

- elaborate mathematical weighting optimization,
- TAM models for every identity,
- exhaustive competitor databases,
- scraping every marketplace,
- statistical normalization of Strategy Advisor scores,
- elaborate brand-portfolio matrices,
- automated strategy pipelines,
- dozens of strategic dimensions,
- revenue forecasts without customer data,
- speculative multi-year brand architecture built before an entry wedge earns traction.

These may become useful later if repeated execution earns the need.

Their current opportunity cost is delaying real customer learning.

# 22. Success Criterion

The Strategy Advisor methodology succeeds when it produces a small portfolio of defensible, differentiated commercial hypotheses worth testing — while also recognizing when a strong entry wedge could unlock a larger coherent brand world — and does so quickly enough that real market behavior remains the primary source of truth.

It does **not** succeed merely because it produces a sophisticated ranking or an elegant long-term brand theory.

The company's operating model is:

> **Research broadly. Test intelligently. Let winners earn depth. Let successful wedges earn expansion. Replace losers. Keep learning.**
