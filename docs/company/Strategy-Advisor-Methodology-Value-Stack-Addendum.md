# Strategy Advisor Methodology — T-Shirt Value Stack Addendum

## Purpose

This addendum extends the current Strategy Advisor Methodology with a qualitative lens for evaluating how an identity-signaling T-shirt can create customer value beyond signaling alone.

It does **not** replace the core company thesis:

> **We sell identity, not apparel.**

It clarifies how additional product value should be interpreted after identity-signaling potential has been established.

---

## Core Rule: Identity First; Secondary Value Multiplies It

Identity signaling remains the primary value proposition and the gate for Strategy evaluation.

The first question remains:

> **Can the right customer plausibly think “that’s me,” “that’s my thing,” “that reflects how I see myself or the world,” or “that represents something I genuinely care about”?**

If identity resonance is weak, secondary product value should not be used to rescue the opportunity.

The operating rule is:

> **Do not use secondary T-shirt value to compensate for weak identity resonance. Use secondary value to amplify strong identity resonance.**

Strategy should therefore think about commercial potential hierarchically:

1. **Identity-signaling fit is the gate.**
2. **Secondary T-shirt value layers are multipliers.**
3. **Market evidence determines whether the combined proposition deserves deeper investment.**

---

## T-Shirt Value Stack

After establishing plausible identity-signaling value, Strategy should ask:

> **What additional value can this T-shirt naturally provide to the customer?**

Potential secondary value layers include:

### 1. Participation Utility / Apparel Fit

For activity- or hobby-based identities:

> **Is a T-shirt naturally useful apparel while participating in, preparing for, gathering around, traveling for, or otherwise engaging with the activity?**

This is stronger than merely asking whether a person *can* wear a T-shirt during the activity.

The important question is whether the shirt gains practical value because it naturally belongs in the participation context.

Examples may include gardening, hiking, camping, fishing, garage / car work, woodworking, pottery, disc golf, geocaching, and similar activities.

When participation utility is high, one product can perform two jobs simultaneously:

- identity signaling, and
- useful participation apparel.

Potential commercial effects include:

- stronger purchase justification,
- higher wear frequency,
- greater repeat-purchase potential,
- more natural ownership of multiple shirts,
- replacement demand,
- stronger gifting utility,
- greater visibility among fellow participants.

Participation Utility / Apparel Fit is a **commercial multiplier, not a prerequisite**. Some strong identities may have little direct participation utility and still support attractive identity-signaling apparel.

### 2. Aesthetic / Fashion Value

> **Would the customer plausibly want to own or wear the shirt because it is aesthetically desirable in addition to what it signals?**

A strong design can create incremental product value through typography, composition, visual taste, styling, fit with the customer’s aesthetic world, and general desirability as clothing.

Fashion value should amplify a resonant identity proposition rather than substitute for one.

### 3. Social / Conversational Value

> **Does the shirt help other people recognize the wearer, create insider recognition, start conversations, create humor, or produce a useful social signal?**

This is related to identity signaling but can create an additional reason to wear the product, especially in settings where other identity holders are present.

### 4. Emotional / Experiential Value

> **Does the shirt evoke a meaningful feeling, memory, ritual, worldview, aspiration, or sense of being understood?**

This can include nostalgia, pride, belonging, restoration, humor, remembrance, or emotional recognition.

### 5. Giftability / Situational Value

> **Can another person readily think “this is so them,” and does the shirt fit a natural gifting or commemorative occasion?**

Giftability can broaden the buying audience beyond direct identity holders and create additional purchase occasions.

Other value layers may be documented when evidence supports them. Do not create new permanent categories merely because a one-off example exists.

---

## Strategic Interpretation

The Strategy Advisor should treat the value stack as a qualitative lens inside Pass 1, not as a new numerical scoring system.

Do **not** add a seventh primary score at this stage.

The existing six Pass 1 dimensions remain the comparison framework:

- Identity-to-Purchase Strength,
- Market Evidence,
- Competitive Opportunity,
- Creative / Product Depth,
- Founder Execution Advantage,
- Testability / MVP Fit.

The value-stack lens should inform written commercial judgment, especially Identity-to-Purchase Strength, Creative / Product Depth, Market Evidence interpretation, and overall Strategic Disposition.

If repeated R021–R040 analysis shows that one value layer consistently changes opportunity ranking or predicts downstream market behavior, Strategy may later recommend formalizing it as a scored subfactor or dimension. Until then, keep it qualitative.

---

## Pass 1 Required Qualitative Check

For each researched identity, after the six-dimension assessment, add a brief section:

### T-Shirt Value Stack

Address only layers that appear materially relevant.

At minimum, state:

- **Identity-signaling gate:** Strong / Plausible / Weak, with brief rationale.
- **Participation Utility / Apparel Fit:** High / Medium / Low / Not material, where relevant.
- **Other meaningful value layers:** aesthetic/fashion, social/conversational, emotional/experiential, giftability/situational, or another evidence-supported layer.
- **Commercial interpretation:** whether these layers materially strengthen the opportunity without substituting for weak identity resonance.

Avoid scoring every secondary value layer numerically.

## Community Analyst Role Boundary

The Community Analyst should not be asked to decide whether an identity is a good T-shirt market.

Where relevant, Community Research should collect descriptive evidence about:

- what participants actually wear,
- where participants gather,
- whether ordinary T-shirts naturally appear in participation settings,
- whether apparel is visible among peers,
- whether identity holders already use apparel for public signaling,
- recurring gifting, event, club, meetup, travel, or commemorative contexts.

The Community Analyst describes the evidence.

The Strategy Advisor interprets its commercial significance.

---

## Candidate-Prioritization Use

When deciding which identities deserve Community Analyst research, Strategy may use the following lightweight pre-research screens:

1. **Identity plausibility** — does this plausibly function as part of self-concept?
2. **Recognition density** — are there likely rituals, behaviors, language, tensions, competence signals, objects, or insider truths?
3. **Participation Utility / Apparel Fit** — for activity identities, can the T-shirt itself plausibly gain practical participation value?
4. **Fragmented-niche / AI+POD fit** — could this be commercially meaningful while remaining awkward or uneconomic for conventional brands to serve deeply?
5. **Strategic learning value** — would researching the identity teach the company something meaningfully different from the existing portfolio?

These are **research-prioritization screens**, not evidence that an identity is commercially attractive.

Community Research and subsequent Strategy analysis still determine whether the opportunity deserves a market test.

---

## Guardrails

- Identity signaling remains the primary value proposition.
- Secondary value layers are multipliers, not substitutes.
- Do not choose an identity mainly because a T-shirt is practical for the activity if identity resonance is weak.
- Do not choose an identity mainly because stylish designs could theoretically be created for it.
- Do not confuse general apparel utility with identity-to-purchase evidence.
- Do not turn the value stack into a large weighted scoring model before market evidence earns that complexity.
- Do not assume high Participation Utility / Apparel Fit causes marketplace demand; treat that relationship as a hypothesis until repeated evidence supports it.

---

## Working Hypothesis to Observe Across R021–R040

A current Strategy hypothesis is:

> **Identity-signaling apparel opportunities may become more commercially attractive when the same T-shirt also delivers meaningful secondary value, especially when it serves as natural participation apparel for the underlying activity.**

A related observed pattern from Round 1 is that several highly competitive activity identities — including gardening, hiking / outdoors, and garage / car culture — also have strong natural T-shirt participation fit.

This pattern is **not yet treated as causal proof**. R021–R040 should be used to gather additional evidence about whether Participation Utility / Apparel Fit consistently corresponds with stronger identity-apparel markets or commercial potential.
