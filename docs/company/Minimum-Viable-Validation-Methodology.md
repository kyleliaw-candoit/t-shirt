# Minimum Viable Validation Methodology

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the company's first working methodology for **Minimum Viable Validation (MVV)**.

MVV exists to insert real customer behavior between AI-generated hypotheses and deeper business investment.

The company can research, strategize, position, and create at very low marginal cost with AI. That increases the risk of an internally coherent but commercially false conclusion.

MVV therefore creates a deliberate reality check:

> **AI can generate the hypothesis. The market must supply the evidence.**

The objective is not to prove a business idea with statistical certainty before launch.

The objective is to obtain the **cheapest credible external evidence** that justifies the next increment of investment.

MVV should reduce market-indifference risk without recreating the cost, delay, or complexity of a full storefront launch.

---

# 2. Operating Principle

The company follows this sequence:

> **Research → Strategy → Brand → Creative → MVV → Evidence Review → Scale / Iterate / Retire**

The corresponding investment rule is:

> **Do not build more infrastructure than the current evidence has earned.**

A successful MVV does not prove that an identity, brand, or design will become a durable business.

It earns the right to run the next, more expensive test.

A failed MVV does not automatically disprove the entire identity opportunity.

It tells the company that one or more hypotheses failed to earn additional investment under the conditions tested.

---

# 3. MVV Is a Methodology, Not a New AI Role

At version 0.1, MVV is a **cross-functional operating methodology**, not a sixth specialized AI role.

This avoids premature organizational complexity before the company has run enough real experiments to understand the work.

Responsibilities remain with the existing roles:

- **Creative Director** creates distinct testable creative hypotheses and iterates creative execution.
- **Brand Manager** owns positioning, meaning, target identity, and brand-level interpretation.
- **Strategy Advisor** interprets portfolio-level commercial evidence and may recommend continued investment, reframing, deprioritization, or retirement.
- **Knowledge Librarian** preserves experiment records, separates local outcomes from reusable knowledge, and updates methodologies only after evidence warrants it.
- **Founder** approves material spending, major strategic decisions, and final go / iterate / retire decisions when judgment is required.

If execution later shows that MVV itself has become a recurring bottleneck requiring distinct expertise, the company may create a dedicated role then.

---

# 4. The Hypothesis Hierarchy

MVV must distinguish what is actually being tested.

The company uses four nested hypothesis levels.

## 4.1 Community / Identity Hypothesis

> **This identity represents a commercially addressable group with meaningful willingness to express or purchase around the identity.**

This is the broadest hypothesis.

It should require the strongest and most repeated evidence before being retired.

---

## 4.2 Positioning Hypothesis

> **This brand meaning or point of view resonates with the target identity strongly enough to support commercial expression.**

Examples include a worldview, tension, recognition mechanism, or customer-facing promise.

A positioning hypothesis can fail while the underlying identity opportunity remains viable.

---

## 4.3 Creative Territory Hypothesis

> **This specific meaning territory can be translated into products that create recognition and desire.**

Examples include insider behavior, discovery, competence, nostalgia, counter-identity, ritual, or another durable creative territory.

A territory can fail while the positioning remains viable.

---

## 4.4 Design / Execution Hypothesis

> **This particular execution is strong enough to win attention and intent from the target customer.**

This includes the actual composition, slogan, illustration, typography, mockup, headline, and other execution choices.

A single weak design is evidence primarily about that design.

> **One bad creative should kill the creative before it kills the community.**

---

# 5. Core MVV Principles

## 5.1 Escalate Commitment Gradually

Customer evidence becomes stronger as the customer gives up more value.

A useful intent ladder is:

> **Attention → Engagement → Click → Contact Information → Purchase / Deposit**

The company should prefer the least expensive signal that is credible enough for the current decision.

---

## 5.2 Prefer Behavioral Evidence Over Stated Preference

Likes, upvotes, comments, and compliments can be useful qualitative evidence.

They are weaker than behavior that costs the customer something.

Clicks cost attention.

Email signups cost information and future inbox access.

Purchases cost money.

MVV should therefore distinguish **resonance** from **buying intent**.

---

## 5.3 Prefer Comparative Evidence Before Universal Benchmarks

At this stage, the company does **not** treat external rules such as:

- 2.5% CTR,
- 15% email opt-in,
- 1.5% kill threshold,
- 10 pre-sales,
- $20 or $50 test budgets

as permanent company policy.

These values may be useful starting hypotheses, but performance varies by audience, platform, placement, offer, price, landing experience, and creative format.

During early MVV cycles, the company should emphasize controlled relative comparisons such as:

> **Which concept materially outperforms the others under comparable conditions?**

After enough comparable company experiments accumulate, the Knowledge Librarian may propose company-specific benchmark ranges.

---

## 5.4 Do Not Confuse Test Failure With Opportunity Failure

Weak results may be caused by:

- weak creative,
- weak positioning,
- weak mockup or presentation,
- pricing,
- landing-page friction,
- poor targeting,
- insufficient traffic,
- channel mismatch,
- technical execution,
- or a genuinely weak identity opportunity.

Evidence Review must diagnose the most plausible failure level before escalating a negative conclusion upstream.

---

## 5.5 Credibility Before Kill Decisions

A negative result is only strategically meaningful when the test was credible enough to support the conclusion.

Before recommending retirement, ask whether the test had:

- a clear hypothesis,
- appropriate target audience,
- sufficient exposure for the decision being made,
- functioning tracking,
- a fair price / offer presentation,
- representative creative quality,
- comparable conditions where comparison is intended,
- enough repeated evidence to distinguish a pattern from one weak execution.

The Strategy Advisor should **not** issue an automatic identity-level kill order based on one design's CTR or one small-budget test.

---

## 5.6 Learning Is a Required Output

Every MVV produces two outputs:

### Operational Output

What should happen to the tested concept now?

Examples:

- advance,
- iterate,
- retest,
- hold,
- retire.

### Knowledge Output

What did the company learn that should improve future decisions?

Examples:

- an effective recognition mechanism,
- a recurring creative failure mode,
- a target-audience distinction,
- a pricing sensitivity,
- a landing-page issue,
- a channel-specific behavior,
- a reliable internal benchmark.

Knowledge should not be generalized beyond what the evidence supports.

---

# 6. Three-Tier MVV System

The default MVV sequence contains three progressively stronger tiers.

Not every experiment must use every tier mechanically. The objective is to obtain enough evidence for the next decision at minimum reasonable cost.

---

## Tier 1 — Organic Resonance

### Objective

Determine whether the identity signal and creative idea produce authentic recognition among relevant people.

### Possible Evidence

- unsolicited positive reactions,
- comments that reveal self-identification,
- saves or shares,
- “where can I get this?” behavior,
- “I'd wear this” statements,
- direct questions about availability,
- useful negative reactions,
- language customers use to describe why the concept does or does not fit.

### Important Limitation

Tier 1 is primarily **qualitative resonance evidence**.

It is not sufficient by itself to establish buying intent.

The company should not create fixed rules such as “three buy comments = validated” without accounting for audience size, exposure, selection effects, and context.

### Ethical / Community Guardrail

Do not spam communities or disguise market research in ways that violate community rules or trust.

Community access is an asset; validation should not damage it.

---

## Tier 2 — Paid Comparative Intent Test

### Objective

Determine which creative or positioning hypotheses earn disproportionately stronger attention and downstream intent under reasonably comparable conditions.

### Default Structure

Test a small set of meaningfully different concepts, normally **3–5** when practical.

Hold major conditions as constant as reasonably possible, including:

- target audience,
- placement,
- approximate spend or exposure,
- call to action,
- offer framing,
- landing-page experience.

Change the hypothesis intentionally rather than changing many variables at once.

### Minimum Funnel To Observe

Where technically practical, capture:

> **Impression → Click → Landing Visit → Email / Waitlist Intent → Checkout / Purchase Intent**

### Interpretation

Early company tests should focus first on **relative performance and diagnostic patterns**, not only absolute external benchmarks.

Examples:

- high relative CTR + high downstream intent → strong candidate to advance,
- high CTR + weak landing intent → attention works; diagnose offer, positioning, price, or landing experience,
- low CTR across one creative territory → Creative Director should revise or retire that territory before Strategy concludes the identity is weak,
- low CTR across multiple credible territories → escalate diagnosis toward Brand Manager and, only after repeated credible evidence, Strategy Advisor.

### Budget Philosophy

Use the smallest budget that can produce decision-relevant evidence.

Do not hard-code a universal dollar amount before the company has learned its own traffic economics.

---

## Tier 3 — Real-Money Test

### Objective

Determine whether people in the intended market will exchange actual money for the product at a credible price.

This is the strongest early validation signal.

### Possible Implementations

- pre-order,
- reservation with payment or deposit,
- minimal product page with live purchase capability,
- limited production run,
- POD fulfillment where setup complexity remains low.

### Principle

> **The goal is not to build the full store. The goal is to make a genuine purchase possible.**

A polished multi-product storefront, large catalog, elaborate automation, or broad brand infrastructure should not be prerequisites for the first real-money signal.

### Interpretation

A small number of genuine purchases can justify the next increment of investment, but should not be treated as proof of durable product-market fit.

The company should capture:

- price presented,
- traffic source,
- audience definition,
- conversion behavior,
- cancellations / refunds where relevant,
- customer comments or objections,
- fulfillment friction,
- unit economics sufficient to understand whether success could become commercially useful.

---

# 7. Evidence Review and Routing

After an MVV experiment, diagnose the evidence at the narrowest justified level.

| Evidence Pattern | Primary Owner | Default Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| One design underperforms | Creative Director | Iterate or retire the design |
| Multiple executions in one territory underperform | Creative Director | Reassess the creative territory |
| Attention is strong but downstream intent is weak | Brand Manager + Creative Director | Diagnose positioning, offer, price, presentation, or landing experience |
| Multiple credible creative territories underperform | Brand Manager + Creative Director | Reassess positioning before escalating upstream |
| Multiple positioning approaches fail credible tests | Strategy Advisor | Reassess opportunity thesis |
| Opportunity repeatedly fails credible commercial tests | Strategy Advisor + Founder | Consider deprioritization or retirement |
| Reusable experimental pattern appears | Knowledge Librarian | Record and determine whether methodology should change |

The table defines default routing, not an automatic decision engine.

# 8. Decision Vocabulary

MVV uses five default dispositions.

## Advance

Evidence is strong enough to justify the next, more expensive stage.

## Iterate

The underlying hypothesis remains plausible, but execution should change before additional investment.

## Retest

The current evidence is inconclusive because the test was weak, noisy, underpowered for the decision, or confounded.

## Hold

Evidence is neither strong enough to advance nor sufficiently negative to justify immediate retirement, and another opportunity currently deserves resources more.

## Retire

Repeated credible evidence indicates that the tested hypothesis no longer deserves near-term investment.

**Retire must name the hypothesis level being retired.**

Examples:

- retire Design B,
- retire the “expert collector” creative territory,
- retire Positioning Hypothesis 2,
- retire the Antiques Treasure Hunters opportunity.

Avoid ambiguous statements such as “the test failed” when only one layer actually failed.

# 9. MVV Experiment Record

Every material MVV should create a lightweight experiment record.

The record should contain:

## Experiment ID

Unique identifier.

## Opportunity / Brand

The identity opportunity and customer-facing brand under test.

## Date

Test period.

## Hypothesis Level

Use one of:

- Community / Identity,
- Positioning,
- Creative Territory,
- Design / Execution.

## Hypothesis

What specifically the company believes may be true.

## Decision This Test Supports

What decision will change based on the evidence.

## Concepts / Variants

What was compared.

## Audience

Who saw the test and how they were targeted or reached.

## Channel

Where the test ran.

## Spend / Exposure

Cost and/or number of meaningful exposures.

## Offer

Price, CTA, landing experience, preorder terms, or other relevant customer proposition.

## Metrics

Only metrics relevant to the hypothesis and decision.

Possible examples include:

- impressions,
- CTR,
- CPC,
- landing visits,
- signup / waitlist conversion,
- checkout starts,
- purchases,
- purchase conversion,
- customer acquisition cost,
- qualitative comments.

## Results

Observed outcomes without interpretation.

## Interpretation

What the evidence likely means and what remains uncertain.

## Credibility Check

Why the test is or is not strong enough to support the proposed decision.

## Disposition

Advance / Iterate / Retest / Hold / Retire.

## Next Action

Specific next step and owning role.

## Knowledge Candidate

Any finding potentially worth preserving beyond this experiment.

## Technical Delivery and Knowledge Capture

An instrumented MVV must plan implementation, environment isolation, database migration, deployment, QA,
approval, and evidence capture before paid traffic begins. Use
`docs/company/MVV-Technical-Delivery-Playbook.md` as the reusable delivery companion.

After a material MVV phase completes, preserve experiment-specific facts in its project records and retrospective.
The Knowledge Librarian should then classify lessons as:

- promote to working standard;
- pilot before standardizing;
- keep on the scaling watchlist; or
- do not add yet.

Do not copy project resource IDs, deployment history, or tagged test values into company methodology.

# 10. Benchmark Calibration

The company should build internal benchmarks from its own experiments.

During early tests, record candidate thresholds as **hypotheses**, not policies.

Example:

- expected strong CTR: hypothesis,
- expected weak CTR: hypothesis,
- expected email signup rate: hypothesis,
- expected purchase conversion: hypothesis,
- expected cost per meaningful signal: hypothesis.

After a sufficient number of reasonably comparable tests, the Knowledge Librarian should review distributions and propose internal benchmark bands such as:

- weak,
- typical,
- strong,
- exceptional.

Benchmarks should be segmented when materially necessary by factors such as:

- channel,
- audience type,
- campaign objective,
- price point,
- funnel stage,
- traffic temperature,
- creative format.

Do not create false precision from a small number of experiments.

---

# 11. First Calibration Opportunity — R012 Antiques Treasure Hunters

R012 Antiques Treasure Hunters will serve as the company's **first MVV calibration case**.

The current brand hypothesis is:

**Worth the Detour**  
*For people who see what others miss.*

The purpose of the first R012 MVV cycle is twofold:

1. evaluate actual creative and commercial hypotheses for R012, and
2. learn how the company's MVV methodology behaves in real execution.

The company should **not** design the R012 experiment before Creative Director has produced concrete, meaningfully different creative hypotheses.

Once those concepts exist, MVV should be designed around the actual decision they create.

Initial R012 thresholds should be recorded as hypotheses and calibrated through execution rather than treated as permanent company policy.

---

# 12. Relationship to Strategy Advisor Methodology

The Strategy Advisor Methodology states:

> **Strategy decides what deserves a test. The market decides what deserves depth.**

MVV operationalizes the second half of that rule.

The Strategy Advisor should use credible MVV evidence when deciding whether an opportunity should:

- receive deeper investment,
- remain in the active test portfolio,
- be reframed,
- be held,
- or be retired.

Identity-level retirement should require evidence proportionate to the breadth of the conclusion.

---

# 13. Relationship to Brand Manager Methodology

The Brand Manager creates a **brand hypothesis**, not a finished immutable brand.

MVV provides external evidence about whether the positioning, recognition mechanism, language, and offer actually resonate.

Brand Manager should revisit brand decisions when evidence points to a brand-level problem.

Brand Manager should not reopen positioning merely because one design underperformed.

---

# 14. Relationship to Creative Director

Creative Director should produce **meaningfully different creative hypotheses**, not a large pile of cosmetic variants.

MVV results should improve creative judgment over time.

Creative Director should learn:

- which recognition mechanisms earn attention,
- which territories create downstream intent,
- which visual or verbal executions fail,
- which customer reactions reveal stronger creative truths.

The objective is not to optimize solely for clicks.

The objective is to create products people genuinely want to own.

---

# 15. Relationship to Knowledge Librarian

The Knowledge Librarian owns the distinction between:

- **experiment result** — what happened once,
- **working pattern** — what appears to recur,
- **validated operating knowledge** — what is stable enough to change methodology.

A single MVV should normally be preserved as an experiment record without immediately rewriting company doctrine.

Methodology should change when repeated execution reveals a useful, stable improvement.

Negative evidence is an asset when it prevents the company from repeating the same mistake.

---

# 16. Stop Rules

MVV should stop when enough evidence exists to make the current decision.

Do not continue testing merely to make a conclusion feel mathematically perfect.

Likewise, do not escalate into expensive infrastructure merely because a cheap signal looks encouraging.

At every stage ask:

> **What is the next decision, and what is the cheapest credible evidence required to make it better?**

This is the governing question of Minimum Viable Validation.
