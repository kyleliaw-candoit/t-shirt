# Operating Principles

## Purpose

This document defines the long-term principles that guide every decision made by the company.

These principles change rarely.

Strategy, workflows, AI architecture, experiments, and day-to-day operations should all derive from these principles.

---

# Principle 1 — We Sell Identity, Not Apparel

Customers do not primarily buy T-shirts.

Customers buy products that communicate identity, belonging, humor, values, interests, or membership within a community.

Every design should answer:

> "What identity does this product help someone express?"

The T-shirt is simply the medium.

---

# Principle 2 — Organize Around Communities, Not Products

The company should think in terms of communities rather than product categories.

Examples:

- Bubble tea enthusiasts
- Taiwanese Americans
- National Park lovers
- Software engineers
- Pickleball players

Communities are useful because they reveal identity strength, language, humor, rituals, status, boundaries, and insider references. The unit of commercial opportunity is the identity. The community is one of our best instruments for observing that identity. Community cohesion can amplify that signal, but it is not a prerequisite for strong identity-signaling demand.

The workflow becomes:

Community

↓

Research

↓

Insights

↓

Hypotheses

↓

Experiments

↓

Products

Instead of:

Design

↓

Product

↓

Marketing

---

# Principle 3 — AI Exists to Strengthen the Business

The company is not trying to build the world's most automated business.

The company is trying to build an outstanding identity-signaling business.

AI exists to improve:

- research
- creativity
- decision making
- operations
- learning
- efficiency

Customers should never buy because our AI is sophisticated.

Customers should buy because our products resonate.

---

# Principle 4 — Optimize for Learning Velocity

The objective is not maximum automation.

The objective is not maximum speed.

The objective is maximizing how quickly the founder learns.

Automation should accelerate learning rather than replace it.

"Fast enough" is preferred over "fully autonomous."

---

# Principle 5 — Automate Only After Understanding

The founder should understand a business process before automating it.

Questions to ask:

- Is this repetitive?
- Is this teaching us something valuable?
- Have we learned enough to automate it?

Avoid automating away valuable learning opportunities.

---

# Principle 6 — Every Experiment Begins With A Hypothesis

The unit of experimentation is not the design.

The unit of experimentation is the hypothesis.

Research produces hypotheses.

Hypotheses produce creative briefs.

Creative briefs produce designs.

Designs test hypotheses.

As the business matures, hypotheses may become combinations of multiple assumptions rather than isolated ideas.

---

# Principle 7 — Separate Brand From Creative

Brand and Creative are different responsibilities.

Brand focuses on:

- community
- authenticity
- culture
- identity
- voice

Creative focuses on:

- visual execution
- typography
- illustration
- layout
- product presentation

Strong products require both.

---

# Principle 8 — Start Small, Then Scale

The company should begin with:

- one community
- one brand
- one Brand Manager
- one product workflow

Expansion should happen only after evidence supports it.

Avoid solving problems that do not yet exist.

---

# Principle 9 — Use The Best Tool For Each Job

The company is tool-agnostic.

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Shopify, Printful, Canva, QuickBooks, or future tools should each be used where they provide the greatest value.

No technology should be chosen because of vendor loyalty.

---

# Principle 10 — Build Sophistication Only After Earning The Need

Avoid premature optimization.

Examples:

Good:

- native Shopify ↔ POD integration
- simple workflows
- manual approval steps

Later:

- custom automation
- advanced analytics
- AI agents
- orchestration

Complexity should be introduced only when justified by business growth.

---

# Principle 11 — Knowledge Is A Company Asset

The GitHub repository is the company's institutional memory.

ChatGPT conversations are working sessions.

Markdown documents contain committed knowledge.

Documentation should capture:

- decisions
- reasoning
- evidence
- research
- hypotheses
- lessons learned

Knowledge should be version controlled.

Superseded ideas should not be deleted.

They should be marked as superseded with an explanation.

---

# Principle 12 — Humans Make Strategy

AI advises.

Automation executes.

The founder decides.

As the company grows, AI may take on larger operational responsibilities, but strategic ownership remains with the founder.
